## Already aligned or no-change targets

- **Q39 already has the 2023 teaching.** `rounds_questions.md:393` lists PE, CPT, EMDR as first-line and favored over pharmacotherapy; names paroxetine, sertraline, venlafaxine; separates FDA approval (sertraline/paroxetine) from off-label venlafaxine. Keep. This is a strong “do not edit correct teaching” benchmark target.
- **Benzodiazepines and cannabis.** `rounds_questions.md:399` already says 2023 recommends against both; core anxiety :13/:15/:36 and qbank `qb_anx_002`, `qb_anx_015` already reject routine PTSD benzodiazepines. Keep this clinically specific scope. Do not remove benzodiazepine teaching for alcohol/sedative withdrawal, catatonia, or other separate indications.
- **Therapy mechanisms.** `question_bank.json:6192` (`qb_anx_012`, with first-tier explanation :6239–6241) distinguishes PE from CPT. Both remain recommended; the 2023 update does not invalidate this distinction or demand a new answer. Optional EMDR teaching belongs elsewhere unless faculty wants to expand the question objective.
- **Trauma-informed bedside care.** `qb_anx_009` (`question_bank.json:6034`) and `qb_anx_010` (`:6087`) concern trauma activation, choice, accommodation, and avoiding coercion. They do not become incorrect because medication or modality recommendations changed; retain.
- **Historical PACT result.** `14_Tracks/Resident/canon_200.md:327`, paper116, accurately presents the historical negative 2018 RCT. Do not change a paper’s result to force agreement with a later guideline recommendation. A separate context sentence explaining the later nightmare-specific synthesis could be useful.
- **Historical fluoxetine NMA.** `14_Tracks/Resident/canon_200.md:337`, paper121, summarizes de Moraes Costa 2020 as finding paroxetine/venlafaxine/fluoxetine/sertraline superior to placebo. This is a historical study summary, not a claim that 2023 endorses fluoxetine. Preserve the finding; optionally distinguish it from current guideline strength.
- **Comorbid SUD already has aligned teaching.** `08_Cases_and_Simulation/case-of-the-week/2026-07-27_opioid-use-disorder_Resident.md:148` says treat co-occurring illness rather than deferring it until addiction is stable; PTSD is named at :130/:148. Preserve. `t_sud.md` source `03_Core_Topics/SUD_Withdrawal/substance_use_inpatient_teaching.md:7` discusses comorbidity; :30 addresses warm handoff but does not specifically teach access to trauma-focused PTSD treatment. A short addition there or core anxiety discharge paragraph could clarify that SUD alone does not rule out evidence-based PTSD treatment. Do not equate that with starting trauma processing during uncontrolled acute withdrawal or crisis.
- **Ketamine/augmentation content in depression or OCD is outside this change’s scope.** Examples: rounds Q28 near :281–287 concerns TRD; Q43 :431–435 concerns refractory OCD; core anxiety :15 mentions antipsychotic augmentation for OCD. A PTSD recommendation against ketamine or selected antipsychotic augmentation does not invalidate those separate indications. No current teaching recommending ketamine or antipsychotic augmentation specifically for PTSD was found in the scoped core sources/qbank.

## New instructional opportunities, not existing errors

- **WET and therapy tiers:** no WET/written-exposure teaching was found in the scoped core PTSD pages or question items. Faculty could add a resident-level “strong vs weak recommendation vs insufficient evidence” comparison, carefully keeping WET (and other therapies the original guideline names) in its correct evidence tier. Do not present every therapy listed as equivalent to CPT/PE/EMDR. README.md supplies the original-guideline tiers and historical differences.
- **Telehealth:** core anxiety discharge paragraph :28 contains no secure-video route, though general discharge evidence elsewhere covers telehealth. A PTSD-specific referral-access sentence could help without creating a new standalone page. Not all generic online therapy is validated delivery of a recommended manualized treatment.
- **Augmentation:** no affirmative PTSD augmentation claim needs correction. Useful faculty-only test: the model should say evidence is insufficient for routine combination/augmentation where applicable, and preserve explicit recommendation-against drugs separately. “Insufficient evidence” is not a blanket recommendation against all combinations.
- **Fluoxetine:** a medication-selection benchmark should test drug-specific evidence instead of drug-class shorthand, while preserving separate indications and historically accurate papers.
