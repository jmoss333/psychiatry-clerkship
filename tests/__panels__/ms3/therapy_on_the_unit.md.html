In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
The admission itself is a treatment window: every patient gets the non-negotiable three — a personalized safety plan, structured psychoeducation, discharge bridging — and every encounter is a chance for a 5-minute micro-intervention matched to the presentation.
  • Inpatient psychotherapy adds a real, modest benefit (SMD ~0.24-0.43, NNT ~7) and no modality is clearly superior — common factors carry the effect, which is exactly the part an MS3 can deliver from day one.
  • The non-negotiable three for every admission: a personalized, quality-checked safety plan (associated with ~45% fewer suicidal behaviors when paired with follow-up), structured psychoeducation (readmission NNT ~5 in psychosis), and discharge bridging — the first weeks out are the riskiest of the whole episode.
  • Match the move to the presentation: BA micro-dose for the withdrawn, one distress-tolerance skill for the flooded, MI readiness ruler for the ambivalent — and the more acute the disorganization, the more behavioral and supportive the intervention.
On the unit
On the unit
Treatment Safety Communication Family
What to ask
Ask what they used to enjoy that could go on today's schedule, what helps even a little when flooded, and who should be in the room for the family meeting before discharge.
MSE focus
Track engagement, affect regulation, and cognitive load — the more disorganized the sensorium, the more behavioral and supportive the intervention has to be.
Safety
Every admission: a personalized safety plan whose quality is checked (not a pasted template), collaborative lethal-means counseling, and discharge bridging before the highest-risk first weeks out.
What to say
We can use these few days: one skill, one scheduled activity, and one plan for the first week home.
Family/collateral
You can always listen to a family without the patient's permission — consent governs what you share, not what you hear.
Rounds
Name the mechanism, the 5-minute intervention delivered, the patient's response, and the discharge-bridge status.
Exam focus
Safety planning with follow-up beats no-suicide contracts; behavioral activation matches the anergic patient; psychoeducation carries the strongest single-intervention numbers in psychosis.

Test yourself
Test yourself
A 22-year-old woman with borderline personality disorder is admitted after self-injury. By day 3, night staff describe her as 'manipulative,' while her day nurse advocates strongly for extending the admission. The next best step in management is:
Why: This is splitting — the patient's polarized internal world reproduced in the team. The evidence-based response is team-level: shared formulation, a single consistent plan, and normalization of the dynamic so it informs rather than divides care. Exclusive assignment entrenches the split; punitive contingencies escalate risk.
