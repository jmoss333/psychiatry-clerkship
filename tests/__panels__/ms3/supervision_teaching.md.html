In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
You sit in the middle of the supervision chain — earning graduated autonomy from your attending while becoming the first-line supervisor for the MS3 — on the shared skills of explicit expectations, active feedback, and knowing when to ask for help.
  • Entrustment is graded (observe → direct → indirect → oversee); over four weeks, earn indirect supervision on specific activities, not vaguely "get better."
  • Teach with the One-Minute Preceptor (get a commitment, probe reasoning, teach one principle, reinforce, correct) and give feedback via Ask–Tell–Ask.
  • Keep formative feedback distinct from summative evaluation, and treat asking for help as a professionalism competency, not a weakness.
On the unit
On the unit
Team Communication Exam
What to ask
At the start of a task, name the expected level of supervision, what you will do independently, and when you will call for help.
MSE focus
Teach students to support MSE claims with observed behavior, direct quotes, and what changed from yesterday.
Safety
Supervision boundaries are patient-safety boundaries; uncertainty, high-risk communication, restraint, legal status, and medication refusal need escalation.
What to say
Use Ask-Tell-Ask for feedback and the One-Minute Preceptor to make the learner reason before you teach.
Family/collateral
Coach students to get permission, ask a focused question set, avoid over-disclosure, and return with a 30-second update.
Rounds
Model a concise assessment, then give one reinforcing point and one correction after the student presents.
Exam focus
Use teaching moments to connect bedside reasoning with board-style distinctions without turning rounds into a lecture.

Test yourself
Test yourself
A student presents a case and you want to teach effectively in one minute. What comes first?
Why: The One-Minute Preceptor starts by getting a commitment so the learner reasons first; you then probe the reasoning and teach to the gap. Leading with the answer, error-listing, or vague praise skips the diagnostic step and teaches less.
