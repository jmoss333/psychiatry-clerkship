In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
Use the interview as a circle with the patient's story at the center — covering all 11 domains in whatever order the conversation allows — and always gather the safety trio (suicide, violence, vulnerability) before closing.
  • Ask suicide questions directly: 'Have you had thoughts of killing yourself?' — direct questions do not implant suicidal thoughts, and indirect approaches miss real risk.
  • MSE language should be behavioral and observable: write 'affect constricted, minimally reactive, mood-congruent' rather than 'patient appeared depressed' — describe what you saw, not your interpretation.
  • Close every interview with a summary in the patient's language, one correction invitation, and one named next step — this closes the loop and builds trust.
On the unit
On the unit
Encounter Communication Safety Family
What to ask
Open with the patient story, then loop through mood, psychosis, anxiety/trauma, substance use, cognition, medical causes, and safety.
MSE focus
Observe from the first moment: engagement, psychomotor activity, speech, affect range, thought form, perception, cognition, insight, and judgment.
Safety
Before closing, ask directly about suicide, violence, grave disability, abuse, and access to means.
What to say
I will ask some direct safety questions because they help us take good care of you here.
Family/collateral
Ask permission when possible; explain confidentiality limits and why collateral helps establish baseline and safety.
Rounds
Report what the patient said, what you observed, what you still need, and what safety questions were answered.
Exam focus
Interview questions test next best question, safety closure, and whether you can distinguish patient report from MSE observation.

During a rounds presentation, a student reports: 'On MSE, mood was depressed and the patient was anxious.' The attending asks for more precise language. Which revision is best?
Why: The MSE separates mood (the patient's subjective report, always quoted in the patient's words) from affect (the clinician's behavioral observation: range, reactivity, congruence). Option 3 shows both correctly. The other options either interpret rather than observe, skip the patient quote, or make a diagnostic statement where a description belongs.
