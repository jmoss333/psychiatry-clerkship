In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
Discharge risk peaks in the first week post-discharge — map every barrier (housing, medications, follow-up, family capacity, means safety) before any note says 'plan for discharge,' and always leave with a scheduled 7-day appointment, not a phone number.
  • Suicide risk in the first week post-discharge is roughly 200× the general-population rate (~2,950 per 100,000 person-years; Chung 2019) — a pre-scheduled 7-day follow-up appointment (not 'they will call to arrange one') is itself an evidence-based intervention.
  • Documented lethal-means assessment was followed by fewer attempts and deaths at 180 days (0.83% vs 3.3%; Boggs 2020, quasi-experimental — an association, not a randomized effect) — 'who can confirm items are secured before discharge?' is a clinical act, not a formality.
  • Family involvement during admission nearly triples the odds of 7-day follow-up, and family psychoeducation lowers 12-month relapse from ~37% to ~10% — bringing family in is treatment, not logistics.
On the unit
On the unit
Family Safety Team Communication
What to ask
Ask who is involved, what support is realistic, what barriers exist, what worries family most, and what safety role they can actually play.
MSE focus
Use patient interaction with family to observe affect regulation, psychosis, insight, conflict patterns, and alliance ruptures.
Safety
Clarify means safety, medication access, supervision, housing, transportation, follow-up, and what would trigger urgent return.
What to say
We can plan better if we name what the family can do, what they cannot do, and what the treatment team remains responsible for.
Family/collateral
Ask better collateral questions: baseline, recent change, risk behaviors, adherence, strengths, barriers, and family limits.
Rounds
Present discharge barriers, family capacity, safety plan, follow-up, and what still must be solved before discharge.
Exam focus
Discharge safety is a systems task; a phone number is not the same as a scheduled follow-up plan.

Test yourself
Test yourself
A patient with a recent serious suicide attempt is being discussed for discharge. The team notes 'family is supportive and patient has a safety plan.' What is the most important missing element before discharge planning proceeds?
Why: 'Family is supportive' is a conclusion, not a plan. The discharge barrier map must verify specific domains: where the patient will sleep, who will be there, whether medications are obtainable, whether a 7-day appointment is actually scheduled, and who has confirmed lethal means are secured. Each unconfirmed assumption is a gap in the safety net.
