In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
Postpartum psychosis is a bipolar-spectrum emergency requiring urgent admission — a lucid interval does not rule it out; distinguish it from PMADs and baby blues by waxing/waning course, and assess safety for both mother and infant on every encounter.
  • Postpartum psychosis typically appears in the first 1–4 weeks, recurs in 30–50% of subsequent deliveries, and is bipolar-spectrum until proven otherwise — screen for prior mania.
  • The EPDS screens for depression, not psychosis — ask directly about delusions and perceptual disturbance, and screen for manic symptoms separately.
  • Stopping maintenance medication in bipolar disorder nearly triples postpartum relapse risk (~66% off medication vs ~23% on prophylaxis) — but 'don't stop everything' is not 'continue everything': valproate is the agent to be off before conception, with lamotrigine or lithium the maintenance options.
On the unit
On the unit
Diagnosis Safety Treatment Family Exam
What to ask
Ask pregnancy/postpartum timing, mood, anxiety, sleep, intrusive thoughts, psychosis, bipolar history, supports, feeding, and medication exposure.
MSE focus
Look for waxing/waning psychosis, disorganization, severe insomnia, anxiety, obsessions, guilt, bonding concerns, and cognitive changes.
Safety
Assess suicide, infant-harm thoughts, psychosis, inability to sleep, neglect risk, domestic violence, and safe caregiving coverage.
What to say
Intrusive unwanted thoughts can be very frightening; I ask directly so we can tell anxiety from psychosis and keep everyone safe.
Family/collateral
Ask partner/family about sleep, behavior change, psychosis, infant safety, medication adherence, and who can provide supervision.
Rounds
Present postpartum timing, PMAD vs bipolar/postpartum psychosis, maternal-infant safety, medication risk/benefit, and support plan.
Exam focus
Postpartum psychosis is an emergency, often bipolar-spectrum, and requires urgent supervised treatment.
Rule out first → first move
Postpartum psychosis (bipolar-spectrum emergency) Perinatal mood or anxiety disorder (PMAD) Baby blues (self-limited, resolves ≤2 weeks) Thyroid dysfunction / infection / eclampsia
First move · Assess safety explicitly for both mother and infant, screen for manic and psychotic symptoms beyond the EPDS, order TSH and infection workup, and obtain collateral about prior mania or prior postpartum episodes.

Test yourself
Test yourself
A patient 8 days postpartum arrives confused and speaking about her baby being 'replaced.' Her family reports she was completely lucid 3 hours ago. She has a personal history of bipolar disorder. Best next step?
Why: Postpartum psychosis is a psychiatric emergency — waxing/waning course with lucid intervals is a feature of the illness, not reassurance. The bipolar history, acute onset, and perceptual disturbance require urgent admission. Starting an SSRI without ruling out bipolar disorder risks destabilization; waiting risks harm to mother and infant.
