In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
A week-by-week map of the four-week inpatient block: safety and the acute workup first, then diagnosis and psychopharmacology, then systems and med-legal, then integration, supervision, and scholarship.
  • Week 1 builds the acute-safety base (suicide, violence, agitation, capacity, withdrawal, delirium, catatonia) while you start carrying patients.
  • Weeks 2–3 deepen diagnosis and prescribing, then move from the patient to the system (commitment, capacity, documentation, disposition).
  • Week 4 is leading rounds, supervising the student, and consolidating — pick a case or topic to present.

On the unit
Safety Diagnosis Team Family
What to ask
Anchor each week to one resident-level job: stabilize acute safety, sharpen diagnosis, manage systems/legal work, then lead and teach.
MSE focus
Track how MSE findings evolve across the admission and how they change level of care, medications, privileges, and discharge readiness.
Safety
Escalate early for suicide, violence, withdrawal, delirium, catatonia, capacity, seclusion/restraint, and involuntary-treatment questions.
What to say
Practice giving patients and families a resident-level explanation of the plan that is clear, bounded, and aligned with attending supervision.
Family/collateral
Make collateral a daily workflow: baseline, risk, medication history, prior admissions, family capacity, and discharge barriers.
Rounds
Lead with an assessment that names uncertainty, risk, and the next decision the team must make.
Exam focus
Use the four-week map to link ward cases to board-style concepts without letting exam prep crowd out clinical ownership.
