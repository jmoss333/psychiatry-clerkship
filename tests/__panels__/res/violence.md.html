Violence Risk (FRST) →
In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
Violence risk is a short-term, dynamic, supervised safety formulation: identify static history, current drivers, target specificity, and the least-restrictive plan for the unit right now.
  • Separate static baseline risk from dynamic drivers such as psychosis, intoxication, withdrawal, pain, agitation, or environmental triggers.
  • Read the escalation curve early: pacing, clenched fists, raised voice, verbal threats, object striking, and blocked exits.
  • Use room setup, team support, de-escalation, and offered oral medication before restrictive interventions when safely possible.
On the unit
On the unit
Safety Communication Family Team
What to ask
Ask about past violence, current target or victim specificity, weapon access, command hallucinations, intoxication/withdrawal, pain, fear, and environmental triggers.
MSE focus
Track arousal, pacing, voice volume, irritability, paranoia, command hallucinations, intoxication, attention, impulse control, and ability to collaborate.
Safety
Interview with team awareness, keep an exit route, reduce stimulation, offer choices and oral medication when safe, and escalate named-target or imminent-risk concerns immediately.
What to say
I want to understand what is making this feel unsafe, and I also need to keep everyone safe while we talk.
Family/collateral
Ask staff, family, and records about prior violence, triggers, weapons, what has helped de-escalate, and any named targets.
Rounds
Present static risk, dynamic drivers, target specificity, unit triggers, de-escalation attempts, and the least-restrictive supervised plan.
Exam focus
Violence risk is dynamic and contextual; past violence matters, but the immediate driver and target specificity determine today's plan.
