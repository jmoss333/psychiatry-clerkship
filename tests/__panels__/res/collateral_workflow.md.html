In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
Collateral is clinical data, not gossip: get permission when possible, ask only what changes diagnosis/risk/treatment/discharge, share the minimum necessary, and bring one clear update back to the team.
  • Start with a one-sentence purpose: baseline/timeline, safety, treatment history, or discharge reality.
  • Use consent and minimum-necessary disclosure; when uncertain, disclose less and ask the supervising team.
  • Return with a 30-second team update: source, baseline, timeline, risk data, discharge barrier/support, and what changes in the plan.

On the unit
Family Safety Communication Team
What to ask
Name the purpose before calling: baseline/timeline, risk, treatment history, or discharge reality.
MSE focus
Use collateral to clarify baseline cognition, behavior, affect regulation, psychosis, sleep, substances, and function.
Safety
Ask about suicide, violence, means access, vulnerability, supervision limits, and what would make discharge unsafe.
What to say
I may not be able to share details without permission, but I can listen and bring relevant information back to the team.
Family/collateral
Ask targeted buckets: baseline/timeline, risk/safety, treatment/function, and discharge reality.
Rounds
Report source, baseline, timeline, risk-relevant data, discharge barrier/support, and what changes in the plan.
Exam focus
Collateral is used to test timeline, risk, capacity, delirium/dementia, mania/psychosis, substance use, and discharge realism.

Test yourself
A student calls a parent for collateral and opens by summarizing the full diagnosis and medication plan before asking any questions. What should the student have done first?
Why: Collateral calls should start with role, purpose, and confidentiality boundaries. The student can gather clinically useful data without over-disclosing protected information.
