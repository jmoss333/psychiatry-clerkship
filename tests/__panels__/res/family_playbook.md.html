In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
Structure the 90-minute meeting across five phases — open/join, share the picture, hear the family and read expressed emotion, problem-solve and plan, then summarize — convene early to plant a seed, not at discharge to harvest one.
  • Convene the family meeting early in the admission — the relapse-prevention benefit of family work accrues over the following 12 months, not on the unit; saving it for the day before discharge squanders the inpatient window.
  • Phase 3 (0:35–0:55) — hearing the family's perspective and reading expressed emotion — is the phase that most changes outcomes; protect the time, listen without correcting in the moment, and de-shame by framing behavior as regulation, not character.
  • Set the meeting goal in one sentence before you begin: 'Align on the diagnosis and discharge plan' or 'Lower the temperature and agree on warning signs' — a meeting with a single clear goal beats one that tries to cover everything.
On the unit
On the unit
Family Communication Team
What to ask
Before the meeting, ask the patient what they want shared, what they fear, and what the family most needs to understand.
MSE focus
During the meeting, observe alliance, expressed emotion, blame, fear, grief, and the patient's ability to participate.
Safety
Clarify confidentiality, means safety, crisis steps, supervision limits, and whether family involvement is helpful or harmful.
What to say
I want to start by making sure we share the same goal: understanding what happened and what needs to be safer after discharge.
Family/collateral
Invite family observations without turning the meeting into cross-examination; ask for examples and changes from baseline.
Rounds
Report meeting goals, what family can support, unresolved conflict, safety commitments, and next system tasks.
Exam focus
High expressed emotion worsens relapse risk; psychoeducation and structured family work can reduce relapse in psychosis.

Test yourself
Test yourself
A family meeting has run to 85 minutes. The team shared the diagnosis and treatment plan thoroughly but skipped Phase 4 (problem-solving) and Phase 5 (close). With 5 minutes remaining, what should the facilitator prioritize?
Why: Phase 5 (close, last 10 min) anchors the meeting: recap the plan in the family's own words, name the follow-up, and hand over a written version. A meeting that ends without a shared next step loses its durability. Over-investing in information delivery at the expense of Phases 3 and 5 is the classic pitfall the page names.
