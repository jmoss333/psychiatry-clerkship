In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
  • Bipolar treatment depends on phase: lamotrigine is not acute antimanic treatment, and antidepressant monotherapy is not recommended for bipolar I depression.
  • Lithium requires timed levels and renal/thyroid monitoring; randomized suicide-prevention evidence is uncertain, so avoid a promise of protection.
  • Clozapine REMS removal did not remove ANC monitoring or the need to watch for severe constipation, myocarditis and other toxicity.

Treatment Safety Communication Exam
What to ask
Ask target symptoms, prior response, adverse effects, medical comorbidities, pregnancy potential, interactions, adherence barriers, and patient goals.
MSE focus
Link medication choice to target findings: psychosis, mania, depression, anxiety, sleep, agitation, cognition, and movement symptoms.
Safety
Check metabolic, QTc, EPS/TD, NMS, serotonin syndrome, lithium toxicity, anticholinergic burden, overdose risk, and monitoring needs.
What to say
Let us choose based on what we are treating and which side effects matter most to you.
Family/collateral
Ask outpatient clinicians/family about prior trials, adherence, side effects, pharmacy access, and what changed after missed doses.
Rounds
Present the target symptom and illness phase, rationale, observed response, adverse effects, monitoring and follow-up plan.
Exam focus
Know signature adverse effects and emergencies: NMS, serotonin syndrome, lithium toxicity, QTc/torsades, and anticholinergic toxicity.
Practice medication ambivalence
Rule out first → first move
Serotonin syndrome (clonus + hyperreflexia + rapid onset) Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (lead-pipe rigidity + hyperthermia + elevated CK) Lithium toxicity (tremor + ataxia + confusion) QTc prolongation / torsades de pointes Anticholinergic toxicity (dry, flushed, delirious, confused)
First move · Clarify the diagnosis, illness phase and target symptom; reconcile medicines, missed doses and interactions; check available monitoring and escalate urgent findings with the supervising clinician.

Test yourself
Test yourself
A patient on a monoamine oxidase inhibitor and a serotonergic tricyclic develops agitation, tremor, visible clonus, and diaphoresis within 2 hours of a dose increase. The intern considers neuroleptic malignant syndrome. What finding best distinguishes these two emergencies?
Why: The discriminating sign is clonus — rapid-onset clonus with hyperreflexia is the fingerprint of serotonin syndrome. NMS presents with lead-pipe rigidity, markedly elevated CK, and hyperthermia developing over hours to days on a dopamine-blocking agent. Both can cause fever, so fever alone does not distinguish them. The MAOI-plus-serotonergic-TCA combination is a classic serotonin syndrome setup.
