In 30 seconds
  • Lead-pipe rigidity + hyporeflexia after a dopamine blocker → NMS; clonus + hyperreflexia, legs>arms, after a serotonergic agent → serotonin syndrome.
  • Dry, flushed skin + mydriasis + urinary retention → anticholinergic toxicity; waxy flexibility/posturing without a clear drug trigger → malignant catatonia (ECT is definitive).
  • Reconstruct the medication history, especially changes in the last 72 hours (NMS can still arise on a stable dose) — that history usually names the syndrome.
On the unit
On the unit
Diagnosis Safety Treatment
What to ask
Ask what changed in the last 72 hours: an antipsychotic started or increased, a dopamine agonist stopped, a serotonergic agent added (SSRI, SNRI, MAOI, tramadol, linezolid, triptans), anticholinergics or antihistamines, recent anesthesia or ECT — and whether it came on over minutes, hours, or days.
MSE focus
Do the two-second exam before anything else: reflexes (lead-pipe rigidity with hyporeflexia vs clonus with hyperreflexia, legs more than arms) and skin (diaphoretic vs dry and flushed); then pupils, mental status, and the vital-sign trend.
Safety
Treat as a medical emergency: hold the antipsychotic and never add another to calm a rigid, febrile patient; cool and hydrate aggressively, send a CK, monitor continuously, and escalate to the medical team early.
What to say
Your fever and stiffness can be a reaction to a medicine, so we are stopping the likely one and treating this as an emergency until you are safe.
Family/collateral
Reconstruct the complete medication history with nursing, pharmacy, and family — every change in the last 72 hours, over-the-counter antihistamines, supplements, and substances — and what the patient looked like before the fever.
Rounds
Present the triad (fever, altered mental status, autonomic instability), the 72-hour medication timeline, the reflex and skin findings that separate NMS, serotonin syndrome, anticholinergic toxicity, and malignant catatonia, the CK, what was stopped, supportive care, and the escalation plan.
Exam focus
Hyporeflexia and lead-pipe rigidity days after a dopamine blocker is NMS; clonus and hyperreflexia hours after a serotonergic change is serotonin syndrome; dry, flushed skin with mydriasis is anticholinergic.
