In 30 seconds
In 30 seconds
Ask directly and specifically — thoughts, plan, intent, preparation, means — then separate chronic from acute risk and turn the answers into a formulation, not a checkbox.
  • Ask in plain words: 'Have you had thoughts of killing yourself?' — direct questions do not plant the idea.
  • Formulate chronic vs acute risk with drivers and modifiable targets — 'denies SI' is not an assessment.
  • Means safety is specific: firearms, stockpiled medication, who secures them, and how that is confirmed.
On the unit
On the unit
Safety Communication Family Team Exam
What to ask
Ask ideation, plan, intent, preparation, past attempts, access to means, protective factors, and what changed today.
MSE focus
Notice hopelessness, agitation, intoxication, psychosis, impulsivity, cognition, and ability to collaborate.
Safety
Formulate acute vs chronic risk and escalate disposition decisions to the supervising team and local policy.
What to say
I ask directly about suicide because it helps us keep you safe; you will not shock me by answering honestly.
Family/collateral
With permission or safety exception, ask about means, recent behavior, prior attempts, supervision, and discharge environment.
Rounds
Present risk state, risk status, drivers, means access, protective factors, and what supervision or discharge barriers remain.
Exam focus
Do not treat scales as disposition tools; risk assessment is a formulation plus supervised plan.

Test yourself
Test yourself
On hospital day 4, a patient admitted after a serious suicide attempt becomes suddenly bright and calm and asks about discharge. Nothing in the treatment has changed. Best next step?
Why: Sudden unexplained improvement after severe suicidality is an explicit escalation trigger — it can reflect relief at a decision to act. A safety plan is one component of the risk plan, not a discharge permission slip, and direct questioning does not implant suicidal thoughts.
