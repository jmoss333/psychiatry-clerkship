Week 6 — Integration, Disposition & Exam Readiness: build a discharge/disposition plan, present a full case with formulation and risk reasoning, and complete the shelf high-yield review and OSCE station set.
  • Skills of the week: the Risk-Stratified Discharge Pathway, the shelf high-yield review with self-check, and the full OSCE station set for stations you have not yet completed.
  • Suggested readings: Felitti 1998 (ACE study) and Deegan 1996 (recovery) — the two papers that frame trauma and the patient's own goals in the discharge plan.
  • Reflection: end-of-rotation professional-identity reflection — what changed in how you think about psychiatric illness, the therapeutic relationship, and your role on the team.

On the unit
Team Exam Family Safety
What to ask
For your final patients, ask what diagnosis still feels uncertain, what risk remains modifiable, and what discharge support is still unproven.
MSE focus
Use serial MSE changes to support your formulation rather than presenting the admission snapshot as static.
Safety
Before discharge discussion, name remaining acute risk, chronic risk, means access, follow-up reliability, and what would change the plan.
What to say
We cannot promise zero risk, but we can name the specific safety steps that must be in place before discharge.
Family/collateral
Confirm the discharge plan with supports: medications, follow-up, means safety, supervision, transportation, warning signs, and what to do if risk escalates.
Rounds
Present a full case with formulation, risk reasoning, family/system plan, and the one uncertainty you would track after discharge.
Exam focus
Use the patient in front of you as the shelf organizer: syndrome, mimic, treatment, safety/legal issue, and disposition.
