# Case of the Week — Bipolar Mania: Recognition & Acute Management

**Format:** ~20–30 min case conference. No pre-reading required.

## Learner-Facing Case Stem

A 24-year-old man is brought to the ED by his roommate after ~5 days of escalating behavioral change. Sleep has collapsed to 1–2 hours nightly without daytime fatigue; he describes his mood as "euphoric and unstoppable" but becomes sharply irritable when redirected. Speech is pressured with clang associations and flight of ideas; thought content includes a grandiose, fixed belief that he has solved the city's transit crisis and been "chosen" to implement it. He has spent beyond his means launching multiple ventures and has been sending pressured 4 a.m. messages to acquaintances.

On exam he is hyperkinetic, hyperverbal, and intrusive, with labile affect and impaired insight/judgment. No fever; HR 104, otherwise normal vitals. He denies substance use and takes no medications. Collateral: a discrete major depressive episode ~12 months ago (≈2 months of anergia, hypersomnia, anhedonia, passive death wishes, no treatment) and a maternal aunt with bipolar I disorder.

Consider: he mentions that during this episode he has also felt "wired but on edge," and at times tearful within the same hour — raising the question of mixed features.

## Guided Discussion Questions

**Q1. Establish the DSM-5-TR diagnosis and specifiers. What is the minimum evidence needed to commit to bipolar I?**
*Teaching point:* Criterion A for mania requires a distinct period (**≥1 week, or any duration if hospitalization is required**) of abnormally elevated/expansive/irritable mood **AND** persistently increased goal-directed activity or energy, with ≥3 (or ≥4 if mood is only irritable) B-criteria symptoms and marked impairment/psychosis/hospitalization. **A single manic episode is diagnostic of bipolar I** regardless of prior depression. Push residents to specify: **with psychotic features** (mood-congruent grandiose delusion), and to interrogate the **"with mixed features"** specifier given the intra-episodic tearfulness/dysphoria — mixed features carry higher suicidality and switch risk and shape drug selection. Note DSM-5-TR retains mania as diagnostically primary and folds prior "mixed episode" into the specifier framework.

**Q2. Build the differential beyond the obvious, including secondary mania.**
*Teaching point:* Beyond stimulant intoxication and hyperthyroidism, residents should generate: **antidepressant/steroid/dopaminergic-induced mania**, **secondary ("organic") mania** from frontal/temporal or right-hemisphere lesions, MS, epilepsy, HIV/neurosyphilis, autoimmune/limbic encephalitis (e.g., anti-NMDA-R), and delirium. Late/atypical age of onset, focal neuro findings, or a fluctuating sensorium should lower the threshold for **neuroimaging, LP, and autoimmune/infectious workup**. Also weigh **schizoaffective disorder** (requires ≥2 weeks of psychosis absent a mood episode) and cluster-B/ADHD overlaps.

**Q3. What is your workup, and how does it gate pharmacology?**
*Teaching point:* Baseline: UDS, TSH, CBC, CMP (renal/hepatic), **beta-hCG in patients of childbearing potential**; ECG when QT-prolonging antipsychotics or high-risk profiles are in play; consider ammonia if valproate encephalopathy is suspected later. **Renal function anchors lithium** dosing/safety; **hepatic function and CBC anchor valproate**; **pregnancy status is decisive** — **valproate (neural-tube defects, ~9–11% major malformation and dose-dependent neurodevelopmental harm) is avoided in anyone who could become pregnant** unless a pregnancy-prevention programme is in place; **lithium is not in that tier** — the absolute Ebstein risk is far lower than historically cited (on the order of 1 in 1,000–2,000), so lithium is used with contraception and preconception counselling, level monitoring across the trimesters, and fetal echocardiography where indicated, and is rarely stopped abruptly given the high peripartum relapse risk.

**Q4. Monotherapy vs. combination for acute mania — what does the evidence say, and how do you choose?**
*Teaching point:* **CANMAT/ISBD 2018** first-line monotherapy: **lithium, quetiapine, divalproex, asenapine, aripiprazole, paliperidone, risperidone, cariprazine**; first-line **combinations** = lithium or divalproex **+** aripiprazole/asenapine/quetiapine/risperidone [1]. **Kishi et al. network meta-analysis (72 double-blind RCTs, n≈16,442)**: aripiprazole, asenapine, carbamazepine, cariprazine, haloperidol, lithium, olanzapine, paliperidone, quetiapine, risperidone, tamoxifen, valproate, and ziprasidone all beat placebo for response; however, only **aripiprazole, olanzapine, quetiapine, and risperidone** had better acceptability (all-cause discontinuation) than placebo, and **topiramate was worse** [2]. Practical drivers: **symptom severity and psychosis** (favor combination and an antipsychotic), **mixed features** (favor SGAs/valproate over lithium; avoid antidepressants), **prior response**, **metabolic/QTc/EPS risk tolerance**, and **maintenance intent**. Combination therapy generally yields faster/greater response at the cost of tolerability.

**Q5. Where do lithium and valproate each earn their place — mechanistically and by evidence?**
*Teaching point:* **Lithium**: unique **anti-suicidal signal** and robust maintenance/anti-manic efficacy; mechanisms implicate **inhibition of GSK-3β and inositol monophosphatase** with downstream neurotrophic effects. **Valproate**: an overview of systematic reviews found valproate superior to placebo across all phases, comparable to lithium on most acute-mania outcomes, similar to quetiapine, but **less effective than risperidone**, with conflicting head-to-head data vs. olanzapine [5]. **Carbamazepine** is effective for mania but a strong **CYP inducer** (autoinduction, drug–drug interactions) and generally lower-tier. Emphasize serum-level–guided dosing and the interaction landscape (NSAIDs/thiazides/ACE-I raise lithium; valproate inhibits lamotrigine metabolism — doubling lamotrigine levels).

**Q6. Address agitation and involuntary treatment in a way that is safe and rights-respecting.**
*Teaching point:* Stepwise, least-restrictive: environmental de-escalation → offered oral SGA ± benzodiazepine → IM options if imminent risk. Residents should articulate **capacity assessment, civil-commitment criteria, and the ethics/documentation of involuntary medication** for a patient whose insight is impaired by illness. Keep dangerousness discussion oriented to **recognition, escalation, and safety planning** — not method detail. Flag heightened suicide risk in **mixed features** and post-mania depressive switch.

**Q7. Design the maintenance plan and adherence strategy from day one.**
*Teaching point:* Agents effective acutely are generally continued into maintenance [1]. **Kishi et al. maintenance NMA (41 RCTs, n≈9,821)**: most active treatments beat placebo for any-episode recurrence; **quetiapine, lithium, olanzapine, aripiprazole+valproate** among those reducing depressive relapse, while lamotrigine is stronger for **depression prevention than mania** [3]. A real-world cohort showed **guideline-concordant first-line regimens cut 30-day rehospitalization (OR 0.21) and any readmission (OR 0.39)** vs. no treatment [4] — a compelling lever for shared decision-making, LAI consideration in poor adherence, psychoeducation, and relapse-signature planning.

**Q8. What are you monitoring longitudinally, and how do you sequence if first-line fails?**
*Teaching point:* Track **serum levels (lithium, valproate), renal/thyroid/parathyroid (lithium), CBC/LFTs (valproate), metabolic panel/weight/lipids and EPS/QTc (antipsychotics)**. For inadequate response: optimize dose/level and adherence, then **switch within or across first-line classes** or **move mono→combination**; second/third-line options (e.g., ECT for severe, treatment-refractory, catatonic, or pregnancy-relevant mania) per CANMAT/ISBD [1]. Reinforce that **lamotrigine and antidepressant monotherapy are not acute-mania treatments** [1,5].

## Ranked Differential

1. **Bipolar I disorder, current episode manic, severe with psychotic features; assess "with mixed features" specifier** — the anchor diagnosis.
2. **Substance-induced (stimulant) or medication-induced mania** — cocaine/methamphetamine, steroids, dopaminergics, antidepressant switch; exclude with UDS/history.
3. **Secondary/"organic" mania** — CNS lesion (frontal/right-hemisphere), MS, epilepsy, HIV/neurosyphilis, autoimmune (anti-NMDA-R) encephalitis; escalate workup if atypical onset or focal signs.
4. **Primary psychotic disorder (schizoaffective/schizophrenia)** — pursue if psychosis persists ≥2 weeks independent of mood; episodic mood-congruent course argues against here.
5. **Hyperthyroidism/thyrotoxicosis** — TSH screen; agitation, insomnia, tachycardia overlap.
6. **Delirium** — fluctuating attention/sensorium; less likely with clear consciousness and normal vitals/exam, but always considered for acute neuropsychiatric change.

## Workup & Management Summary

**Workup:** UDS; TSH; CBC; CMP (renal + hepatic); **beta-hCG if applicable**; ECG when using QT-prolonging agents; neuroimaging/LP/autoimmune-infectious panel if age/onset/exam atypical.

**Acute management:**
- **Safety & legal:** dangerousness and capacity assessment; least-restrictive containment; civil commitment/involuntary medication where criteria are met.
- **Discontinue** antidepressants/stimulants; correct precipitants.
- **Pharmacology:** first-line monotherapy (lithium/divalproex/first-line SGA) for moderate mania; **lithium or divalproex + SGA** for severe or psychotic mania [1,2]; agent choice individualized by mixed features, metabolic/QTc/EPS risk, pregnancy status, and maintenance intent.
- **Agitation:** oral SGA ± benzodiazepine, IM if imminent risk.
- **Refractory/severe/catatonic/pregnancy-relevant:** consider **ECT** [1].

**Maintenance & monitoring:** continue effective acute agent [1,3]; serum-level–guided dosing; longitudinal renal/thyroid/metabolic/hepatic/CBC/QTc surveillance; psychoeducation, adherence supports (consider LAI), relapse-signature planning; guideline concordance improves rehospitalization outcomes [4].

## Facilitator Notes (not for the learner handout)

- **Objectives:** (1) apply DSM-5-TR mania criteria including required increased-activity criterion and the mixed-features specifier; (2) generate a robust differential including secondary mania and know when to image/LP; (3) reason from the acute-mania and maintenance network meta-analyses to individualized drug choice; (4) integrate lithium/valproate/carbamazepine mechanism, interactions, and teratogenicity; (5) manage agitation and involuntary treatment ethically; (6) build a maintenance/monitoring plan.
- **Debate-worthy tensions:** monotherapy vs. combination (speed/efficacy vs. tolerability); lithium's anti-suicidal advantage vs. practical monitoring burden; when atypical presentation mandates a neuro workup; LAI timing.
- **Evidence nuance to surface:** in Kishi 2021, breadth of "efficacious" agents is wide, but the **acceptability** filter narrows practical first choices to aripiprazole/olanzapine/quetiapine/risperidone [2]; topiramate underperforms and is not an antimanic agent.
- **Pitfalls to correct:** treating mixed features with antidepressants; lamotrigine for acute mania; valproate in patients who could become pregnant; neglecting capacity documentation.
- **Safety framing:** all suicide/dangerousness content stays at recognition/escalation/safety-planning level.
