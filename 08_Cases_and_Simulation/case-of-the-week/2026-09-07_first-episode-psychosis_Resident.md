# Case of the Week — Resident Version (Week of September 7, 2026)

## First-Episode Psychosis: Workup & Initial Management

**Learner level:** Psychiatry residents (PGY-1–3) · **Format:** ~20–30 min discussion · **This is a fully synthetic, de-identified teaching case. No real patient is depicted.**

---

## Case Stem (learner-facing)

Jordan is a 19-year-old college sophomore with no prior psychiatric history, brought to the ED by his roommate after three months of progressive social withdrawal, academic collapse, and referential and persecutory ideation (foil over the windows "so the transmissions can't get through"; conviction that campus security implanted a tracking device in his phone charger). For two weeks he has experienced two voices conversing about and commenting on his actions.

He is calm, guarded, and cooperative. He denies suicidal or homicidal ideation; the voices have never issued commands. Cannabis use was roughly monthly, discontinued six weeks ago because "it made the voices louder"; symptoms have since worsened. No prescribed medications, no known medical illness. Family history is notable for a maternal uncle "hospitalized for his nerves" in his twenties. His roommate confirms normal baseline function a year ago.

**Vitals:** T 37.0°C, HR 84, BP 118/72, RR 14. Neurologic exam nonfocal; no catatonic signs. MSE: guarded, restricted affect, mildly over-inclusive but linear thought process, third-person commentary hallucinations, persecutory and referential delusions, no formal thought disorder beyond mild tangentiality, poor insight, intact sensorium (MoCA 27/30, attention items intact).

---

## Guided Discussion Questions

**Q1. Defend a working DSM-5-TR diagnosis and specify what would revise it.**

*Teaching point:* Three months of active psychotic symptoms with prodromal decline places him in schizophreniform disorder territory; schizophrenia requires ≥6 months of continuous disturbance (prodrome counts toward the six months, so a careful timeline of the earliest negative symptoms matters — his may already approach criterion). Persistence and worsening ≥6 weeks after cannabis cessation weighs against substance-induced psychotic disorder, which requires remission with sustained abstinence. Schizoaffective disorder requires a mood episode concurrent with psychosis for the majority of illness plus ≥2 weeks of psychosis without mood symptoms — screen longitudinally rather than ruling out at first contact.

**Q2. Design the medical workup: what is evidence-supported as routine, what is targeted, and what is low-yield?**

*Teaching point:* Evidence-informed recommendations distinguish a routine tier (CBC, CMP, TSH, UDS, hCG, HIV, syphilis, B12; baseline BMI, lipids, A1c, prolactin if using a prolactin-raising agent) from clinically guided testing [2]. Routine MRI and EEG in neurologically intact first-episode patients have low diagnostic yield; reserve for red flags (focal signs, seizure semiology, catatonia, rapid cognitive deterioration, atypical age, treatment resistance) [2]. Autoimmune encephalitis (anti-NMDA-receptor) testing is targeted, not universal: consider with subacute onset plus seizures, dyskinesias, autonomic instability, prominent memory deficit, or catatonic features. Discuss ceruloplasmin/24-h urine copper only with hepatic or movement findings.

**Q3. What is the evidence linking duration of untreated psychosis (DUP) to outcome, and how strong is it?**

*Teaching point:* Meta-analysis of 33 samples found longer DUP correlates with worse general symptomatic outcome, more severe positive and negative symptoms, lower remission likelihood, and worse social functioning at long-term follow-up — consistent but modest effects (r ≈ 0.13–0.18), with no association with employment or quality of life [4]. Frame DUP honestly: a modifiable prognostic marker justifying urgency, not a deterministic variable. RAISE-ETP secondary analyses similarly showed treatment effect moderation by DUP, with greater benefit when DUP was below the sample median (~74 weeks) [1].

**Q4. Choose and dose an antipsychotic. What pharmacologic principles are specific to first episode?**

*Teaching point:* First-episode patients show greater D2 sensitivity: response occurs at lower doses (often half the chronic-illness dose), and EPS/metabolic vulnerability is higher. APA guideline: an antipsychotic selected by side-effect profile and patient preference, with metabolic monitoring at baseline and scheduled intervals [3]. Reasonable defaults: risperidone 1–2 mg/day or aripiprazole 5–10 mg/day, titrated to the minimum effective dose; olanzapine is discouraged first-line in youth given metabolic burden. Counsel on akathisia explicitly — it is commonly misread as worsening agitation and drives nonadherence. Adequate trial = 2–6 weeks at therapeutic dose before switching; two failed adequate trials (including at least one SGA) should trigger a clozapine discussion — delayed clozapine initiation is a recognized system failure in early psychosis.

**Q5. The family asks about "the program for young people with psychosis." What does coordinated specialty care (CSC) actually deliver, and what is the evidence?**

*Teaching point:* CSC bundles shared decision-making, low-dose pharmacotherapy, individual resilience-focused therapy, family psychoeducation, supported employment/education, and case management [5,6]. RAISE-ETP (34 community sites, cluster-randomized, n=404) found CSC superior to usual community care over 2 years on quality of life and total symptoms, with greater gains at shorter DUP [1]. Adjunctive CBT for psychosis in first-episode populations has a Cochrane evidence base that remains limited in quality — beneficial signals on some outcomes but low-certainty evidence overall — worth knowing when setting expectations [7].

**Q6. He asks, "Do I have to take this forever?" How do you counsel on treatment duration and relapse risk?**

*Teaching point:* Honest framing: after remission from a first episode, guideline consensus supports continuing the antipsychotic for at least 1–2 years, with discontinuation associated with high relapse rates; any taper should be slow, collaborative, and paired with early-warning-sign monitoring and rapid re-engagement plans [3]. Avoid both false certainty ("lifelong, period") and false reassurance. Document the shared decision. This is also the moment to consolidate cannabis abstinence counseling — continued use predicts relapse and poorer response.

**Q7. What is your safety assessment and disposition framework for this presentation?**

*Teaching point:* Elevated suicide risk in early psychosis concentrates in the first years of illness and around insight gains post-stabilization. Assess: command hallucinations (absent here), persecutory delusions with identified persecutors, hopelessness as insight emerges, and access to means as part of collaborative safety planning. He currently denies ideation, has engaged family, and is treatment-engaged — reasonable for discharge to urgent (days, not weeks) CSC intake with family involvement and an explicit written safety plan naming warning signs, coping steps, contacts, and crisis resources. Escalate to admission for command hallucinations, ideation, inability to care for self, or absent support. Keep documentation at the level of recognition, protective factors, and escalation thresholds.

## Ranked Differential Diagnosis

1. **Schizophreniform disorder → schizophrenia** (timeline-dependent) — prodrome + 3 months active symptoms, family history, age-typical.
2. **Substance-induced (cannabis) psychotic disorder** — argued against by persistence/worsening ≥6 weeks post-abstinence; still relevant as a course modifier.
3. **Psychotic disorder due to another medical condition** — autoimmune encephalitis, thyroid disease, neurosyphilis, HIV, B12 deficiency, temporal lobe epilepsy; screen per tiered workup [2].
4. **Schizoaffective disorder / mood disorder with psychotic features** — requires longitudinal mood characterization; not supported cross-sectionally.
5. **Delusional disorder** — persecutory content, but prominent hallucinations and functional decline exceed criteria.
6. **Attenuated psychosis syndrome / CHR-P** — no; threshold symptoms present. Listed to prompt discussion of the clinical-high-risk construct in siblings/roommate concerns.

## Workup & Management (consolidated)

- **Tier 1 (all patients):** CBC, CMP, TSH, UDS, HIV, RPR/treponemal, B12, ±hCG; baseline BMI, lipids, A1c; AIMS; prolactin if indicated [2,3].
- **Tier 2 (clinically guided):** MRI brain, EEG, autoimmune panel (serum + CSF anti-NMDAR if suspicion), ceruloplasmin, heavy metals — red-flag driven [2].
- **Pharmacotherapy:** low-dose SGA, minimum effective dose, 2–6 week adequate trials, early clozapine pathway after two failures [3].
- **Psychosocial:** CSC referral with warm handoff [1,5,6]; CBTp adjunct with calibrated expectations [7]; family psychoeducation; supported education re-entry.
- **Prognosis communication:** DUP-outcome evidence [4]; relapse-prevention framing; cannabis abstinence.
- **Safety:** structured risk assessment, written safety plan, defined escalation thresholds; urgent-interval follow-up.

## Facilitator Notes (do not distribute to learners)

- **Level targeting:** PGY-1s should drive Q1–Q2; PGY-2/3s should carry Q3–Q6. Push residents to commit to numbers (doses, trial durations, monitoring intervals) rather than abstractions.
- **Discussion traps worth springing:** (a) "order MRI on everyone" — make them defend yield; (b) anti-NMDAR testing indiscriminately vs. targeted; (c) conflating akathisia with agitation and raising the dose; (d) never mentioning clozapine in a first-episode discussion.
- **Systems angle:** ask who in your institution takes CSC referrals, what the wait time is, and what the resident's role is in bridging — the evidence is only as good as the handoff.
- **Safety content:** keep all dangerousness discussion at recognition/escalation/safety-planning level; no method detail in any role-play.
- **Timing:** Q1–Q2 (10 min), Q3–Q5 (12 min), Q6–Q7 (8 min).
