# Case: Two Adequate Trials Down — Now What? — Resident

## Major Depressive Disorder: Treatment Selection & Augmentation

*Case of the Week — 2026-10-05 · Psychiatry resident version*
*De-identified synthetic teaching case. Based on articles retrieved from PubMed; see numbered references with DOI links at the end.*

---

## Stem (learner-facing)

**Part 1.** A 46-year-old man with recurrent MDD (three prior episodes, first at age 28) is referred to your resident clinic. His current episode has lasted seven months. Trial history this episode: escitalopram 20 mg × 10 weeks (full adherence, confirmed by pharmacy fill data) with ~20% MADRS improvement, then cross-titration to venlafaxine XR, at 225 mg for the past 9 weeks, with partial benefit — MADRS has fallen from 34 to 24. He still has prominent anhedonia, mid-insomnia, impaired concentration (he is on a performance-improvement plan at work), and passive suicidal ideation without plan or intent, which he reliably reports and which has not progressed; he has no prior attempts and no access to firearms. PHQ-9 today is 16. He asks about "that nasal spray ketamine thing" his coworker mentioned.

**Part 2 (revealed during discussion).** MDQ negative; no family history of bipolar disorder. No psychotic features. BMI 31, A1c 5.9%, mild hypertension on lisinopril. Normal TSH, CBC, CMP; no OSA symptoms on STOP-BANG beyond BMI. Drinks 2–3 beers on weekends; THC/other substances denied, UDS negative. Prior episode remitted on escitalopram; longest well interval 4 years. He had 8 sessions of CBT two years ago that he found "somewhat useful" and stopped.

---

## Guided discussion

**Q1. Does this man have treatment-resistant depression? Defend your answer with a definition — and name what else must be excluded before applying the label.**
→ *Teaching point:* The most common operational definition of TRD is **failure of ≥2 adequate antidepressant trials** (adequate dose, duration ≥6–8 weeks, confirmed adherence) within the current episode — which he meets only if the escitalopram trial counts as "failed" (<25–50% improvement) despite the venlafaxine partial response. CANMAT 2023 prefers the framing **"difficult-to-treat depression"** and emphasizes excluding pseudo-resistance first: adherence (confirmed here), bipolarity (MDQ negative, but probe longitudinally — MDQ sensitivity is imperfect), comorbid substance use, OSA, hypothyroidism, and personality pathology shaping the symptom picture [1]. Definitions matter practically: they gate access to esketamine and trial eligibility.

**Q2. Venlafaxine has produced a real partial response (MADRS 34→24). Optimize, switch, or augment — and what does the evidence actually say about that choice?**
→ *Teaching point:* With a **partial response at 9 weeks**, switching discards accrued benefit; augmentation is generally preferred (CANMAT: augmentation favored after partial response to a first- or second-line agent; switching favored after no response or intolerance) [1]. VAST-D: aripiprazole augmentation produced higher remission than switch to bupropion (28.9% vs 22.3%, RR 1.30) and higher response rates, at the cost of akathisia, somnolence, and weight gain [3]. Note VAST-D's population (predominantly older male veterans) when generalizing. Dose optimization (venlafaxine to 300–375 mg) is defensible but has a flat dose-response above ~225 mg for most patients; noradrenergic effects and BP burden rise.

**Q3. Rank the augmentation options for this specific patient — mechanism, expected effect size, and the monitoring each commits you to.**
→ *Teaching point:* First-tier evidence: **atypical antipsychotics** (aripiprazole 2–10 mg, brexpiprazole, quetiapine XR; partial D2 agonism/5-HT2A antagonism) — best-replicated but metabolically costly in a man with BMI 31 and A1c 5.9% (prediabetes); commit to weight, A1c/glucose, lipids, AIMS monitoring. **Lithium** (0.5–0.8 mEq/L) — older but durable evidence, mostly with TCAs; adds anti-suicidal signal; needs renal/thyroid monitoring, Li level checks, interaction caution with his lisinopril (ACE inhibitors raise lithium levels). **T3 (25–50 mcg)** — modest evidence (STAR*D step 3 comparison), benign monitoring. **Bupropion combination** — mechanistically complementary (NDRI), helpful for anhedonia/fatigue, weight-neutral; in VAST-D the weakest of the three arms, but a reasonable fit here [3]. A recent synthesis integrating meta-analytic and early head-to-head data positions lithium, quetiapine, and esketamine as the best-supported post-first-line options, without a clear single winner [5]. There is no high-quality evidence ranking these *for this phenotype*; model explicit trade-off reasoning for the group.

**Q4. He asked about esketamine. Walk through eligibility, the ESCAPE-TRD data, and the practical/regulatory burden.**
→ *Teaching point:* Intranasal esketamine (NMDA-receptor antagonist; glutamatergic burst → synaptogenesis) is approved for TRD **as monotherapy or with an oral antidepressant** (monotherapy since January 2025), and — **with an oral antidepressant** — for depressive symptoms in adults with MDD with acute suicidal ideation or behavior. ESCAPE-TRD (open-label, rater-blinded phase IIIb, n=676) randomized TRD patients to esketamine NS vs quetiapine XR, each on top of an ongoing SSRI/SNRI: esketamine produced faster and more frequent remission, and early response predicted long-term outcome — ~90% of week-4 responders achieved remission at some point by week 32 [6]. Caveats to teach: open-label design with subjective endpoints, industry sponsorship, and quetiapine XR as a comparator many patients tolerate poorly. Practical burden: REMS enrollment, 2-hour monitored sessions (sedation, dissociation, BP), twice-weekly induction, driving restrictions, cost. Reasonable to sequence *after* a conventional augmentation attempt, or earlier if severity/functional collapse demands speed [1,5,6].

**Q5. How does his passive suicidal ideation shape the plan — level of care, agent selection, and the safety plan you document today?**
→ *Teaching point:* Risk formulation: chronic passive SI, no plan/intent, no attempt history, no access to firearms, treatment-engaged, employed, future-oriented → outpatient management appropriate, with explicit escalation criteria. Agent selection implications: lithium carries an anti-suicidal evidence base; esketamine does **not** — its label states that effectiveness in preventing suicide or reducing suicidal ideation or behavior has not been demonstrated, its acute-suicidality indication rests on improvement in *depressive symptoms*, and it does not replace hospitalization, means safety, or safety planning (do not transfer off-label racemic IV ketamine data to esketamine); avoid large supplies of lethal-in-overdose agents (TCAs; venlafaxine is itself more toxic in overdose than SSRIs — dispense sensibly). Document a collaborative safety plan: warning signs, internal coping, social contacts, clinical and crisis contacts (below), means counseling, and the specific threshold for stepping up care (active SI, plan, intent, command hallucinations, functional collapse). Reassess at every visit and at every treatment transition — early antidepressant changes are a known period of risk elevation.

**Q6. STAR*D is twenty years old. What does it still buy you prognostically for this man, and where should you not over-apply it?**
→ *Teaching point:* STAR*D's step-wise remission decay (36.8% → 30.6% → 13.7% → 13.0%; cumulative ~67%) remains the best large-scale map of sequential *treatment steps* — not of monotherapy alone: from level 2 on, steps mixed switches and augmentations (level 3 offered a mirtazapine or nortriptyline switch, or lithium or T3 augmentation), and patients chose which strategies they would accept. The ~14% level-3 rate is therefore pooled across strategies: it tells you each further step after two failures yields less, not that switching specifically fails [2]. Limits: the pooled step rates cannot rank switch against augment; open-label, no placebo, QIDS-based endpoints, enrolled before modern augmentation standards — it cannot adjudicate between today's augmentation options; use it for trajectory counseling, not agent selection.

**Q7. He is 46 — but if this same picture presented at 72, how would OPTIMUM change your reasoning?**
→ *Teaching point:* OPTIMUM (NEJM 2023, n=742, age ≥60, TRD): aripiprazole **augmentation** outperformed **switching** to bupropion for well-being and remission (remission ~29% vs ~19%), without higher fall risk in the aripiprazole arm [4]. Geriatric translation: augmentation ≥ switch even in older adults; start aripiprazole at 2 mg; weigh akathisia, parkinsonism, orthostasis, and polypharmacy. General lesson: age alone should not push you toward the "gentler-looking" switch strategy — it may simply be less effective.

## Differential (ranked, with discriminating features)

1. **MDD, recurrent, moderate-severe, with inadequate response to 2 trials (TRD / difficult-to-treat depression)** — leading diagnosis; partial venlafaxine response.
2. **Bipolar II depression, missed** — MDQ negative but keep longitudinal vigilance; recurrent early-onset episodes are a soft flag. Would reframe entire pharmacologic plan.
3. **MDD with inadequately treated comorbidity driving "resistance"** — prediabetes/metabolic burden, possible undetected OSA (BMI 31 — consider formal sleep evaluation if snoring/unrefreshing sleep emerge), weekend alcohol (below threshold but monitor).
4. **Persistent depressive disorder with superimposed MDE ("double depression")** — ask about inter-episode subsyndromal symptoms; changes psychotherapy emphasis and prognosis counseling.
5. **Medication-contributed symptom burden** — venlafaxine-related insomnia/BP effects mimicking depressive somatic symptoms; lisinopril relevant only as a lithium interaction, not causally.

## Workup & management

- **Re-verify adequacy** of both trials (dose, duration, adherence — done via pharmacy data; model this habit).
- **Labs/monitoring baseline for chosen augmenter**: aripiprazole/quetiapine → weight, A1c, lipids, AIMS; lithium → Cr/eGFR, TSH, Ca, ECG >40 yo, level at 5–7 days (mind lisinopril interaction); T3 → TSH.
- **Measurement-based care**: MADRS or PHQ-9 every visit; define the decision point in advance (e.g., reassess augmentation at 4 and 8 weeks; exit if <20% improvement at 4) [1].
- **Psychotherapy**: restart structured CBT or switch to behavioral activation targeting anhedonia; combination therapy outperforms medication alone in difficult-to-treat depression [1].
- **Sequencing scaffold** (CANMAT-consistent): optimize/augment venlafaxine now (aripiprazole 2–5 mg or bupropion 150–300 mg given metabolic profile — make the group argue it) → if inadequate at 8 weeks, lithium or quetiapine XR vs esketamine-plus-OAD [1,3,5,6] → rTMS or ECT discussion if two augmentation strategies fail, with ECT prioritized for psychosis, catatonia, nutritional compromise, or acute safety concerns.
- **Safety**: documented safety plan (see Q5); follow-up ≤2 weeks after any regimen change; explicit escalation criteria in the note.

## Facilitator notes (not for learner handout)

- **Timing**: 25–30 minutes. Q2–Q4 are the core; compress Q6–Q7 if short.
- **Push residents past guideline recitation**: require a committed recommendation with dose, monitoring plan, and pre-specified exit criteria. "Augment with an atypical" is not a plan; "aripiprazole 2 mg, reassess MADRS at week 4, stop if akathisia or <20% improvement, A1c at baseline and 12 weeks" is.
- **Evidence-appraisal thread**: VAST-D (male VA population), OPTIMUM (geriatric), ESCAPE-TRD (open-label, industry, quetiapine comparator) — each generalizes imperfectly to this patient; make that explicit rather than treating trial results as interchangeable [3,4,6].
- **Common resident errors**: serial switching past step 2 without reconsidering diagnosis, adherence, and the full option set (STAR*D shows falling yield per step, not that switching specifically fails); choosing quetiapine in metabolic syndrome without naming the trade-off; forgetting the ACE-inhibitor–lithium interaction; treating esketamine as a last resort *or* a first resort rather than a sequenced option; omitting psychotherapy from "treatment-resistant" algorithms entirely.
- **Safety framing**: keep all suicide-related content at recognition, risk formulation, safety planning, escalation, and means counseling. No method or lethality detail beyond class-level overdose-toxicity reasoning needed for prescribing decisions.
