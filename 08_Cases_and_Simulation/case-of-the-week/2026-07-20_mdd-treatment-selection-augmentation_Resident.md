# Case of the Week — Resident Edition
## Major Depressive Disorder: Treatment Selection, Sequencing & Augmentation

**Date:** 2026-07-20
**Learner level:** Psychiatry residents (PGY 1–4). Assumes DSM-5-TR fluency.
**Format:** ~20–30 min discussion. No required pre-reading.
**Citations:** Based on articles retrieved from PubMed. Full reference list at the end. All patient details are **synthetic and de-identified**.

---

### The Case (learner-facing stem)

A 41-year-old woman is referred to the outpatient clinic for a recurrent major depressive episode, moderate-to-severe, without psychotic features. This is her **third lifetime episode**. Two prior episodes remitted on sertraline. The current episode has lasted ~10 weeks despite the patient's PCP restarting **sertraline, now titrated to 200 mg/day for the past 7 weeks**.

Current symptoms: depressed mood, anhedonia, initial and terminal insomnia, psychomotor slowing, guilt, impaired concentration, and 4 kg weight loss. She endorses passive suicidal ideation without plan or intent. No history of mania or hypomania on structured review; no psychotic features; no active substance use disorder (occasional alcohol). Medical history: migraine, mild obesity (BMI 31), no cardiac disease. She is worried about weight gain and sexual side effects and asks whether "there's a genetic test that tells you which one will work."

Baseline QIDS-C is 15 (moderate). TSH, CBC, CMP unremarkable. She is engaged, works full-time, and has good social support.

---

### Guided Discussion Questions

**Q1. Is this an adequate first-line trial, and how do you stage her treatment resistance?**
*Teaching point:* Sertraline 200 mg × 7 weeks is an **adequate dose and duration** — this is a genuine non-response, not pseudoresistance. Because it's her first adequately dosed antidepressant *this* episode, she is at the threshold of **Stage I treatment resistance**; a second adequate failure would meet the common ≥2-trial definition of **treatment-resistant depression (TRD)**. Probe residents on the limits of TRD staging systems (Thase-Rush, Maudsley) — heterogeneous definitions complicate the augmentation-trial literature and generalizability of network meta-analyses [5,6].

**Q2. Switch, augment, or optimize — how does the evidence adjudicate, and what does VAST-D actually show?**
*Teaching point:* She is already near-maximal on sertraline, so "optimize" is largely exhausted. The **VAST-D** RCT (n=1,522, predominantly male VA sample) compared switch-to-bupropion vs. augment-with-bupropion vs. augment-with-aripiprazole after ≥1 failed antidepressant: **aripiprazole augmentation** yielded higher remission (28.9% vs. 22.3%; RR 1.30, 95% CI 1.05–1.60) and response (74.3%) than switching, but with more akathisia, somnolence, and weight gain [2]. Key nuances to draw out: modest absolute benefit (~6.6% remission difference), the male-predominant sample limits generalizability to this patient, and the weight-gain signal is directly relevant given her BMI and stated concerns.

**Q3. Rank augmentation agents by evidence, and reason about mechanism.**
*Teaching point:* Network meta-analyses of augmentation in TRD support **atypical antipsychotics** (aripiprazole, quetiapine, brexpiprazole, cariprazine) and **lithium** as among the better-evidenced options, with esketamine now a distinct adjunct [6,7,8]. Mechanistic framing:
- **Aripiprazole/brexpiprazole/cariprazine** — D2/D3 partial agonism + 5-HT1A partial agonism/5-HT2A antagonism; lower metabolic burden than olanzapine/quetiapine but **akathisia** is dose-limiting.
- **Quetiapine XR** — 5-HT2A antagonism and, via norquetiapine, NET inhibition; sedating, metabolically active.
- **Lithium** — augments serotonergic neurotransmission; the classic augmentation agent with anti-suicidal signal, but requires monitoring and has a narrow therapeutic index.
- **T3 (liothyronine)** — mechanism uncertain (possibly enhances cortical serotonergic tone); modest evidence, well tolerated.
Given her obesity and weight concern, a **low-metabolic-risk partial agonist** (aripiprazole/brexpiprazole/cariprazine) or **lithium** is more rational than quetiapine or mirtazapine.

**Q4. She asks about pharmacogenomic testing. What do you tell her, and what do the data support?**
*Teaching point:* The **PRIME Care** RCT found that pharmacogenomic testing modestly influenced prescribing and produced a **small, time-limited improvement in remission** — the effect was statistically detectable but clinically modest and attenuated over follow-up [9]. The **CPIC guideline** provides actionable dosing guidance where a genotype is *already known* (e.g., **CYP2C19** and **CYP2D6** metabolizer status for SSRIs — poor metabolizers may need dose reduction; ultrarapid metabolizers may underexpose) but does not endorse routine pre-emptive testing to *select* the drug class [10]. Bottom line for the patient: testing can refine dosing and flag metabolism outliers, but it is not a validated "which drug will work" oracle; shared decision-making about efficacy vs. tolerability trade-offs remains primary.

**Q5. When and how do you deploy esketamine/ketamine, and where does it sit in sequencing?**
*Teaching point:* Intranasal **esketamine** is FDA-approved as adjunct for TRD and for MDD with acute suicidal ideation/behavior. A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis supports **efficacy against depressive symptoms and, in the acute setting, suicidality**, with dissociation, sedation, and transient blood-pressure elevation as the main tolerability issues and REMS-mandated monitoring [8]. Discuss sequencing: esketamine is typically reserved after ≥2 oral failures, requires in-clinic administration/observation, and does not replace safety planning. Contrast with racemic IV ketamine (off-label, rapid but access/durability limits).

**Q6. Integrate psychotherapy and relapse prevention into the plan.**
*Teaching point:* For a recurrent (3rd-episode) patient, **relapse prevention is a primary goal**, not an afterthought. The **sequential model** — adding structured psychotherapy (CBT, well-being therapy, MBCT) after pharmacologic response to target residual symptoms — reduces relapse/recurrence in meta-analysis [4]. Residual insomnia and anhedonia are high-yield relapse predictors worth targeting directly. Plan for **maintenance pharmacotherapy** given ≥3 episodes (many guidelines favor indefinite maintenance after ≥3 recurrences).

**Q7. Tailor the regimen to *this* patient's side-effect priorities.**
*Teaching point:* She flags **weight** and **sexual dysfunction**. Systematic side-effect mapping supports **personalized prescribing** [3]. Practical moves: prefer low-metabolic-risk augmentation (partial agonists) or lithium over quetiapine/mirtazapine; if sexual dysfunction is the SSRI-driven complaint on switch, consider **bupropion** (weight-neutral, low sexual dysfunction, but lowers seizure threshold and can worsen anxiety/insomnia — note her insomnia) or **mirtazapine** trade-off (helps sleep/appetite but causes weight gain). The art is negotiating the efficacy–tolerability frontier explicitly with her.

**Q8 (Safety).** She has **passive SI without plan/intent**. Structure the assessment and disposition.
*Teaching point:* Conduct a structured risk assessment (ideation, intent, plan, access to means, protective factors, prior attempts, recent losses), formulate **acute vs. chronic risk**, and build a **collaborative safety plan** with means-safety counseling. Consider lithium's anti-suicidal evidence when weighing augmentation. Keep the teaching oriented to recognition, formulation, and escalation — not method detail. Reassess risk at each visit during cross-titration.

### Ranked Differential / Diagnostic Reconsideration

1. **MDD, recurrent, moderate-severe, unipolar** — best fit; confirm no missed hypomania (collateral, mood chart) before committing to aggressive antidepressant augmentation.
2. **Bipolar II depression** — reconsider given recurrent episodes and treatment resistance; a missed bipolar diathesis reframes the entire strategy (mood stabilizer/atypical over antidepressant monotherapy).
3. **MDD with mixed features** — subthreshold manic symptoms alter drug choice and antidepressant risk.
4. **Persistent depressive disorder with intermittent MDE ("double depression")** — affects chronicity framing and maintenance planning.
5. **Contributory medical/substance factors** — reassess alcohol quantity, sleep apnea (obesity, insomnia, fatigue), and occult hypothyroidism trends despite a normal TSH.

---

### Workup & Management

**Workup / assessment:** Confirm adequacy of prior trials (dose, duration, adherence — verify via pharmacy refill history); structured mania screen and mood charting; measurement-based care (QIDS/PHQ-9 at each visit); metabolic baseline (weight, BMI, lipids, HbA1c) before an atypical antipsychotic; ECG if considering agents with QT liability; pregnancy planning discussion; consider genotype only if it will change dosing decisions.

**Management sequence for this patient:**
1. **Augment** the (adequately dosed) sertraline with a **low-metabolic-risk agent** — aripiprazole/brexpiprazole/cariprazine or lithium — chosen collaboratively around her weight and sexual-function priorities [2,6,7].
2. If augmentation fails or is not tolerated → **switch class** (e.g., SNRI or bupropion), or escalate to **esketamine** adjunct given emerging TRD status and passive SI [8].
3. Add **structured psychotherapy** (sequential model) for residual symptoms and relapse prevention [4].
4. **Reserve ECT** for severe, refractory, catatonic, psychotic, or high-acuity presentations; discuss rTMS as a non-pharmacologic option for moderate TRD.
5. **Maintenance:** given ≥3 lifetime episodes, plan long-term/indefinite maintenance and monitor for residual-symptom relapse predictors.

### Facilitator Notes (not for the learner handout)

- **Time:** ~30 min. Anchor on Q2–Q4 (switch/augment adjudication, agent ranking, and the honest limits of pharmacogenomics) — these separate resident-level from student-level reasoning.
- **Evidence caveats to surface:** VAST-D's male-predominant VA sample [2]; heterogeneity and shifting TRD definitions across augmentation NMAs [6,7]; pharmacogenomics' modest, attenuating effect in PRIME Care and the distinction between drug *selection* and dose *refinement* per CPIC [9,10]; esketamine's REMS/access and durability questions [8].
- **Metabolic stewardship:** use her BMI to make the trade-offs concrete — this is where "match the drug to the patient" [3] becomes a real prescribing decision, not a platitude.
- **Bipolarity vigilance:** recurrent + treatment-resistant depression should always re-trigger a bipolar rule-out before piling on antidepressant augmentation.
- **Safety framing (Q8):** structured formulation, means-safety, and escalation only — no method detail. Reassess across cross-titration.
- **Optional extension:** number-needed-to-treat vs. number-needed-to-harm for aripiprazole augmentation; how to present that trade-off to a patient in plain language.
