# Case of the Week: Chronic Lithium Toxicity at a "Therapeutic" Level — Resident Version

## Lithium — Monitoring, Toxicity, and Interactions: Case of the Week (2026-11-09)

*De-identified synthetic teaching case. No real patient details. Based on articles retrieved from PubMed.*

---

## Stem (learner-facing)

**Stage 1.** A 58-year-old man with bipolar I disorder, maintained on lithium carbonate 1200 mg nightly for 19 years, is referred from his PCP's office for "altered mental status." Six weeks ago his PCP started hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg and lisinopril 10 mg for newly diagnosed hypertension. Over two weeks the patient has developed progressive unsteadiness, worsening tremor, word-finding difficulty, and daytime somnolence. He denies extra lithium doses; pill counts corroborate adherence.

Other meds: quetiapine 100 mg qhs, atorvastatin. He mentions months of polyuria and nocturia ×3, with constant thirst.

**Vitals:** T 36.9°C, HR 58, BP 148/84, RR 14.

**Exam:** Drowsy but arousable, inattentive, MOCA deferred. Coarse bilateral tremor, dysarthria, bidirectional nystagmus, limb and gait ataxia, diffuse hyperreflexia without sustained clonus. No rigidity.

**Stage 2 — labs:** Na 149, K 3.4, Cl 110, HCO3 24, BUN 38, Cr 1.9 (baseline 1.2, eGFR baseline ~62), Ca 10.9, TSH 8.2, glucose 104. Urine osm 180 mOsm/kg despite serum osm 312. **Lithium level (12 h post-dose): 1.9 mEq/L** (levels 0.6–0.8 for the past decade). ECG: sinus bradycardia at 56, nonspecific T-wave flattening, QTc 462 ms.

---

## Guided discussion questions

**Q1. Classify the toxicity (acute, acute-on-chronic, chronic) and explain why the category changes both prognosis and management.**

→ *Teaching point:* This is **chronic toxicity**: steady-state body stores with gradually reduced clearance. CNS tissue concentrations are already equilibrated, so neurotoxicity is severe at modest serum levels, symptom resolution lags serum clearance, and risk of persistent sequelae is highest. Acute ingestions in naive patients show the inverse pattern (high levels, GI-predominant, initially mild CNS signs); acute-on-chronic sits between [1,2]. Severity grading should therefore be **clinical**, not level-based [1].

**Q2. Walk through the pharmacokinetic mechanism by which HCTZ and lisinopril each raised his level. Which other common drugs behave this way, and which are comparatively safe?**

→ *Teaching point:* Lithium is freely filtered and ~80% reabsorbed, mostly in the proximal tubule via sodium pathways. **Thiazides** induce distal natriuresis → compensatory proximal sodium (and lithium) reabsorption → levels rise ~25–40%. **ACEi/ARBs** lower efferent arteriolar tone and GFR and promote volume contraction → reduced lithium clearance, an effect that can appear weeks after initiation. **NSAIDs** reduce prostaglandin-mediated afferent dilation. **Loop diuretics** are lower-risk than thiazides but not risk-free, particularly with volume contraction [2,3]. Comparatively safer: amiloride, acetaminophen, aspirin. Any intercurrent volume-depleting illness compounds all of the above. When such agents are unavoidable, pre-emptively reduce lithium dose and recheck levels in 5–7 days [2,4].

**Q3. Interpret the polyuria, Na 149, and dilute urine. How does this complicate his toxicity, and how is it managed?**

→ *Teaching point:* **Lithium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (arginine-vasopressin resistance)** — lithium enters principal cells via ENaC and downregulates aquaporin-2. The resulting free-water loss produces hypernatremia and volume contraction, which further reduces lithium clearance: a self-amplifying loop that likely tipped him over. Management: treat the toxicity; long-term options include once-daily dosing, dose minimization, and **amiloride** (blocks ENaC entry of lithium into principal cells); thiazides "work" for NDI but are a trap in lithium patients for the reasons in Q2 [1,2,3].

**Q4. His level is 1.9 — below many "dialysis numbers." Make the argument for and against extracorporeal removal, referencing accepted decision frameworks.**

→ *Teaching point:* EXTRIP-style criteria favor hemodialysis when level >4.0 mEq/L with impaired renal function, or >2.5 with severe symptoms, or when coma/seizures/life-threatening dysrhythmias are present, or when the expected time to reach safe levels is prolonged [1]. He is below numeric thresholds, but: chronic pattern with severe neurotoxicity, Cr 1.9 and climbing, NDI impairing his own clearance, bradycardia with QTc prolongation. Reasonable practice: aggressive isotonic saline, serial levels q4–6h, nephrology at the bedside, and a low threshold to dialyze if neurologic status worsens or levels fail to fall. Watch for **post-dialysis rebound** from tissue redistribution — recheck levels after each run [1,2].

**Q5. What is SILENT, and what findings here raise your concern for it?**

→ *Teaching point:* **Syndrome of Irreversible Lithium-Effectuated NeuroToxicity**: persistent, mostly cerebellar deficits (ataxia, dysarthria, nystagmus, cognitive impairment) lasting ≥2 months after lithium clearance. Risk tracks with chronic toxicity, duration of neurotoxic exposure, and fever. His cerebellar-predominant picture and two-week symptom duration are exactly the phenotype — another argument for rapid, definitive level reduction [1,3].

**Q6. The labs flag three long-term lithium endocrine/renal complications. Identify each, and summarize the current evidence on long-term renal risk — how worried should we be?**

→ *Teaching point:* (a) **CKD**: baseline eGFR ~62 after 19 years; recent territory-wide cohort data confirm lithium's association with CKD and thyroid dysfunction compared with other mood stabilizers, though progression to ESRD with modern monitoring is uncommon and must be weighed against lithium's unmatched efficacy and anti-suicidal effect [5,6]. (b) **Hypothyroidism**: TSH 8.2 — common, treatable with levothyroxine; not an indication to stop lithium [4]. (c) **Hypercalcemia/hyperparathyroidism**: Ca 10.9 — check PTH; lithium shifts the parathyroid set-point [4]. Real-world monitoring quality is poor — in a Swedish cohort, substantial fractions of lithium-treated patients lacked guideline-concordant level and creatinine checks — so build systematic monitoring into your practice [7].

**Q7. He stabilizes over five days; level 0.4, residual mild ataxia. The team debates permanent discontinuation. Structure the risk–benefit discussion and your monitoring plan if lithium continues.**

→ *Teaching point:* Frame around: 19 years of mood stability on lithium (strongest maintenance evidence in bipolar I, plus anti-suicidal effect) vs. CKD stage 3a, NDI, this neurotoxic event, and age-related decline in clearance [4,5]. Options: (1) resume at reduced dose targeting 0.4–0.6 trough, once-daily nightly dosing, strict interaction rules (no thiazides/NSAIDs; if antihypertensive needed, consider amlodipine), amiloride for NDI, q3-month levels/Cr, sick-day rules in writing; (2) cross-taper to an alternative (e.g., quetiapine optimization, valproate (avoid in reproductive-age patients), lamotrigine for depression-predominant course) accepting meaningful relapse risk after two decades of response. Discontinuation, if chosen, should be slow (>4 weeks) to reduce rebound relapse risk [3,4]. Document shared decision-making.

## Differential (ranked)

1. **Chronic lithium toxicity precipitated by thiazide + ACEi + NDI-driven volume contraction** — subacute cerebellar/encephalopathic picture, coarse tremor, level 1.9 from stable 0.6–0.8, rising Cr. Best fit.
2. **Serotonin syndrome** — hyperreflexia present, but no serotonergic agent, no clonus, subacute (not <24 h) course.
3. **NMS** — on quetiapine, but no rigidity, no fever, no CK elevation pattern; bradycardic rather than autonomically stormy.
4. **Hypercalcemic/hypernatremic/uremic metabolic encephalopathy** — each present in mild degree and contributory; none explains the full cerebellar syndrome.
5. **Posterior-circulation stroke or cerebellar degeneration** — bilateral symmetric findings with a toxic time-course argue against; image if focality or poor recovery.
6. **Hypothyroid encephalopathy** — TSH 8.2 is too modest; contributory at most.

## Workup & management

- **Labs/monitoring:** lithium q4–6h until clearly declining; BMP, Ca/PTH, TSH/fT4, CK, serum/urine osm; strict I/O; telemetry (bradycardia, QTc); baseline cognitive exam to track.
- **Immediate:** hold lithium, HCTZ, lisinopril; isotonic saline resuscitation with careful correction of hypernatremia (free-water deficit correction once volume-replete; avoid overcorrection); potassium repletion.
- **Nephrology:** early consult; hemodialysis per Q4 framework; anticipate rebound.
- **Psychiatry:** daily mental-status checks; relapse-prevention plan before discharge; medication reconciliation letter to PCP covering the interaction cascade.
- **Discharge systems fix:** flag chart for lithium–drug interactions; written sick-day rules; scheduled 5–7 day post-dose-change level; q3-month level/Cr/TSH/Ca while on long-term therapy [4,7].

## Facilitator notes

- **Timing:** 30 minutes. Stage the stem; after Stage 1, force a committed differential and ask "what level do you predict?" — most will guess >3; the reveal (1.9) sets up the chronic-toxicity teaching.
- **Common misconceptions:** level-based severity grading; "therapeutic level rules out toxicity"; thiazides as first-line for lithium NDI without dose adjustment; stopping lithium permanently by reflex without structured risk–benefit analysis.
- **Pivot questions:** "Same level, 25-year-old after acute ingestion — what changes?"; "Which antihypertensive would you have chosen?"; "When would you dialyze despite a level of 1.9?"
- **Evidence nuance for discussion:** long-term renal risk magnitude remains debated — cohort data (e.g., Hong Kong 2025 [5]) show elevated CKD risk vs. comparators, while ESRD remains rare with modern monitoring; contrast with monitoring-quality data [7] showing guidelines are inconsistently followed in practice.
- **Safety framing:** if intentional overdose scenarios come up, keep discussion to recognition, stabilization, observation level, and safety planning — no method or quantity detail.
