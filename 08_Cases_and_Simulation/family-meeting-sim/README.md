# Family Conversation Simulator — authoring guide

`family-meeting-sim.html` (slug `family-meeting-sim.html`, both sites) is a static branching rehearsal of eight family conversations. It has no AI, no voice, no network calls and no free text. Scenario data lives inline in the tool, in `<script type="application/json" id="fms-scenarios">`, so the whole tool is one attestable artifact.

The display title deliberately avoids the phrase "Family Meeting": `tests/fd-search.test.mjs` pins that searching "family meeting" puts the Family Meeting Playbook first, and a tool titled "Family Meeting Simulator" outranks it. Search aliases for the tool avoid that phrase for the same reason.

## Status and attestation

- Every scenario is a **draft** written from fictional composites. The tool ships **pending** behind the attestation gate (`reviewed.json` row: `risk {kind: clinical, level: high}`, `by: "Pending faculty review"`) because it rehearses safety-planning and means-safety roles.
- Only faculty attest, in the faculty console. An agent never signs, promotes or adds a `contentHash`.
- Any edit to the scenario text or scores after attestation drifts the row back to pending. Batch edits so one review covers them.

## How scoring works

- Four axes, each scored 0, 1 or 2 per choice: `validation` (naming the feeling and the concrete event; translations offered as hypotheses), `structure` — "Structure, agenda and consent" (agenda, roles, summary, close, and stating and holding what the patient agreed can be shared), `safety` — "Safety actions" (risk as clinical data routed to the team, asking about firearms and other quick-harm means, specific safety roles, escalating in the moment) and `nonblaming` (illness-not-character framing, holding several perspectives, no cross-examination). A choice earns points on an axis only for content that does that axis's work: kindness, separate-time offers and confidentiality handling never earn `safety`, and a stock phrase ("I understand", "that sounds hard") never earns `validation`. Each axis carries `levels` — what 0, 1 and 2 mean on it — in the data; the same kind of move scores the same tuple in every scenario (deferring to the attending without engaging is V0 S1 Sa0 N1 everywhere).
- A run's score per axis is the points earned divided by the most that the moments actually reached could offer. The debrief shows four bars, the authored feedback for each choice, the strongest authored option where the learner chose a weaker one, and "Try again from this moment". It never shows pass or fail.
- Bands are descriptive: "often" (75% or more), "some of the time" (40–74%), "mostly missing" (below 40%).
- A separate safety gate lists the required safety actions for each scenario and whether they were done, done after a miss, or not done; skill percentages never offset it. A gate item whose label says "route" is satisfied only by a choice that routes: the directive ("you'll need to remove the rifle", "someone must be with her at all times") keeps its one safety point on the bar and never satisfies the item, so the ending's `notMet` addendum renders instead (2026-10-29; before that, the directive counted as done and the ending said the team had it). Endings are composed from what actually happened on the path. Choice order within a node is frozen once shipped; append new choices. The rubric is a draft and unvalidated.
- The strongest option must not be the uniquely longest button. Measured 2026-10-28, it was, at every one of the 69 nodes (mean 67 words against 13.5), so pressing the longest button scored 100% everywhere. `tests/family-meeting-sim.test.mjs` pins the count (`LENGTH_CUE_PIN`: 62 of 70 nodes on 2026-10-29) and it may only fall: when you add or rewrite a node, lengthen a plausible wrong option (a long reassurance, a long list of orders, a long policy explanation) or cut the exemplar to the dialogue, then lower the pin in the same PR.
- The gate renders above the bars as "Safety steps on this run". If a required step was not done, or a safety flag fired, a banner says so and the safety bar's band sentence is replaced; a disclosure beyond consent adds its own notice. A "Run record" line (tool and rubric version, path, earned/max per axis, gate status, flags) is shown on screen for proctored use only — never stored, copied or sent.

## Data model (v2)

- Top level: `version` (2), `rubricVersion` (a string; bump the minor on any score change, the major on any axis or gate definition change), `status`, `axes`, `flagDefs`, `phases`, `scenarios`.
- `flagDefs` is a fixed enum: `suicide-statement-not-asked`, `risk-judged-by-student`, `lethal-means-dismissed` (kind `safety`) and `disclosure-beyond-consent` (kind `consent`). A choice may carry at most one `flag`. A safety-flagged choice scores `safety: 0` and never satisfies a gate item; a disclosure beyond consent scores 0 on every axis and routes to a repair node.
- Each scenario has `gate.required`: items with a stable `id` (`s1-`…`s8-` prefix; never renamed once shipped), a learner-visible `label`, `offeredAt` (node ids), `satisfiedBy` (`nodeId#index` at those nodes) and `claimTerms` (lower-case phrases an ending may only use inside an addendum whose `when` names the item). Every path must offer every item.
- An ending has `title`, a base `text` that is true on every path, and optional `addenda`: `{ "when": { "met": [ids], "notMet": [ids] }, "text": … }`, appended in order when every condition holds (`met` = done, including after a miss; `notMet` = not done).
- A node may carry `branchOf: "<main-line node id>"`: a twin that repeats its parent's `choices` verbatim (the test enforces this) so one route can show something the others did not — S8 `roles` opens with the attending stepping out with Joanne on the keyed route, and `roles-unrouted` is the same moment on the routes where nothing reached the attending. A twin forks the story, never the scoring, and is not on the main line.
- Stable item ids for the rubric-validation study: skill items are `scenarioId/nodeId#index`; gate items are their `id`.

## Adding or editing a scenario

1. Give it a kebab-case `id`, a `title`, `setting`, `time`, `learnerRole`, `participants` (`name`, `role`, `stance`), `goals`, `gate` (at least two required safety actions), `start`, `nodes` and `endings`. Optional: `note` and `noteLink` (`label`, `page` — a slug in `shipped_pages.json`; never a page that may be withheld).
2. Each node has a `phase` (`join`, `share`, `hear`, `plan`, `close` — the five phases of `family_meeting_playbook_90min.md`), `lines` (`speaker`, `text`; speaker `narration` renders as a stage direction), an optional `cue`, an optional `repair: true`, and 3–4 `choices`.
3. Each choice has `text` (what the learner says), `next` (a node id or an ending id), `scores` with all four axes, and `feedback` (one or two sentences: why this move helps or hurts, in the same words the playbook and Family Systems Practice use). Optional `flag` (a `flagDefs` key).
4. Structure: 6–10 decisions on every path; no cycles; every node and ending reachable; every path must touch all four axes and offer every gate item; option 0 at each main-line node is the strongest on every axis, so the option-0 path scores 100% and does every required safety step. Most missteps have a repair node that rejoins the main line; the test pins the choices that still have neither a repair route nor a flag, and that list may only shrink.
5. Content rules: fictional people only; no doses; no instrument items; no hard-coded crisis numbers (the build injects them at `<!-- crisis-block-html -->`); no consent or confidentiality rules for minors (link the ethics and law page instead) — and no confidentiality *promise* to a minor either: the exemplar defers ("what you tell me goes to the team; before anything goes to your grandmother I'd talk with you first about what and why — and some things I'd have to share"), owner decision 2026-10-28; means safety stays at the level of the concept, with details routed to the team and local policy; ask about a gun in the house where no relative volunteers one. For a relative's own risk the learner's scope is ask directly, hear the answer, stay, and hand over to the attending in the moment — never a risk formulation. Asking plan and means questions after a positive answer is scored as the wrong setting, not a risk judgement (one safety point, no flag; owner decision 2026-10-28). A character never refers to "this page": the crisis block is for the learner, so the clinician says "crisis numbers that are yours".
6. Endings: put anything that depends on a safety step in an addendum conditioned on that gate item, never in the base text. The same rule binds the close node's summary: a converging `close#0` may not assert a plan element the path may not have produced ("the adult you named" after no adult was named) — write it conditionally ("whoever you two name") or leave it to the ending's addenda.
7. Run `node --test tests/family-meeting-sim.test.mjs`. The engine refuses to run on data that fails its own checks.

## Storage

One literal key, `cw_family_sim_v1`, on the learner's device: scenario ids, run counts, best percentage per axis and the date of the last run (day only). Nothing else is stored or sent. "Clear my saved progress" removes the key.

## Not in this version

Voice or LLM-driven family members (decision D-F1-3), LLM scoring of free responses, spaced-repetition cards and inclusion in "Export my progress" are deliberately out of scope. The voice prototype lives separately under `_prototypes/sp-interview/` and is not part of this tool.
