# Required Clinical Encounters (LCME 6.2)

**DRAFT for Josh's edit.** The nine encounter types every MS3 student should meet on this
clerkship, each with a minimum level of responsibility and a simulated alternative for a student
who does not meet it on the ward.

**What this list is for.**

- **LCME 6.2 (Required Clinical Experiences):** faculty specify the patient types and clinical
  conditions, the skills students perform, the settings, and the expected level of student
  responsibility. The nine types are the review's §7 starter list; the responsibility levels and
  settings are proposals for Josh to confirm.
- **LCME 8.6 (Monitoring of Completion):** tracking is the school's system, not this library.
  This list is what that system tracks. Students report their progress at the midpoint
  (section 1b of [mid_clerkship_feedback.md](mid_clerkship_feedback.md)).
- **LCME 8.7 (Comparability):** the simulated-alternative column lets a student at any site meet
  every required experience, which is the review's answer to comparability across sites.

**Responsibility levels used below.**

- **Observe:** present for the encounter and able to discuss it; not performing its parts.
- **Participate:** carries out defined parts (for example, the substance history, or the
  collateral call) with the team, which carries the rest.
- **Perform under direct supervision:** carries out the core task (for example, the interview)
  with a supervisor present.

A simulated alternative is a fallback for a missed encounter; it is not a substitute the student
may choose instead of an available ward encounter. When a student does meet the encounter on the
ward, the matching card (last column) documents it.

## Notes for the clerkship director

- **Why these minimums.** The student may interview a psychotic or depressed patient under direct
  supervision from the first days (DO-1 exists for exactly that). Mania, withdrawal, delirium, and
  family work are proposed at *participate*, because the risks there (escalation, medical
  instability, confidentiality) make a defined part the safe minimum. Capacity assessments,
  agitation events, and child cases are proposed at *observe*: the review notes students rarely
  lead capacity conversations, the Week 1 materials tell students not to manage an unsafe
  interaction alone, and an adult inpatient unit may not see a child at all.
- **What the review did not specify.** The review's §7 table gives the encounter type and its
  simulated alternative only. The responsibility levels, settings, and card column above are this
  pack's proposals.
- **Attestation status of the simulated alternatives (as of 2026-09-24).** The Interview Room
  case pack (Dana, Marcus, Ray) is faculty-reviewed. The communication, reasoning, and
  family-practice pages are attested, but the scenarios inside them are still drafts; WP-10 binds
  scenario content to attestation. Three alternatives do not exist yet and are labelled with the
  work package that builds them (WP-11 for the capacity and de-escalation simulations, WP-9 for
  the child cases).
- **An encounter met only in simulation** should be named in the student's end-of-rotation
  record in the school's system, so that the school's monitoring (LCME 8.6) can see which
  experiences were simulated.
