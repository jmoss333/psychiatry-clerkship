What Changed Since Last Quarter
The June 23, 2026 baseline was comprehensive through its review date, but this refresh surfaces several consequential items published earlier in 2026 (Feb–Apr) that the baseline missed, plus genuinely new developments in the eight days since. Framed honestly: this is less “what happened this quarter” and more “what the record now shows, corrected and current.”
CMS issued the FY2027 IPF PPS/IPFQR proposed rule (CMS-1847-P, published April 7, 2026; comment period closed June 1, 2026) — not in the baseline. It is the first rule to spell out IPF Patient Assessment Instrument (IPF-PAI) mechanics: quarterly submission beginning October 1, 2027, five statutory data categories, FHIR-API submission option, and a 100%-of-items/≥80%-of-assessments compliance threshold. Final rule expected ~August 2026.
FDA approved Auvelity (dextromethorphan-bupropion) on April 30, 2026 for agitation associated with Alzheimer’s dementia — the first FDA-approved non-antipsychotic for this indication, and a genuine alternative to brexpiprazole that avoids the antipsychotic-class Boxed Warning. Not in the baseline.
FDA approved Bysanti (milsaperidone) on February 20, 2026 — a new atypical antipsychotic (new chemical entity, iloperidone-bioequivalent) for schizophrenia and bipolar I manic/mixed episodes. U.S. availability expected Q3 2026. Not in the baseline.
SAMHSA issued an April 24, 2026 Dear Colleague Letter further restricting which harm-reduction supplies federal grantees may fund (syringes, direct-distribution fentanyl test strips excluded; naloxone and infectious-disease testing remain fundable), with a companion letter urging that MOUD be framed as a recovery pathway rather than indefinite maintenance. This extends a July 2025 policy shift the baseline did not capture.
The CMS Innovation in Behavioral Health (IBH) Model’s operational status — flagged as “uncertain” in the baseline — is now resolved: active in Michigan, New York, and South Carolina (2026–2027 implementation); a Cohort II funding opportunity closed June 3, 2026, with up to five more states and awards expected September 2026.
IPFQR Psychiatric Inpatient Experience (PIX) survey mandatory reporting is now live for the January 1–December 31, 2026 calendar year (FY2028 payment consequence) — confirms the baseline’s “build the workflow now” flag has become a present-tense compliance requirement.
Joint Commission and NQF announced January 26, 2026 that they are aligning the Sentinel Events List with NQF’s Serious Reportable Events List, effective January 1, 2027. Three legacy workplace-safety sentinel events are retained — Homicide, Sexual Abuse/Assault, and Physical Assault of a staff member — pairing directly with the existing R3 #42 workplace-violence-prevention standard. Not in the baseline.
No change: NICE NG10432 (rapid-tranquillisation rewrite) remains in committee development with no draft published; APA MDD (2010)/Bipolar (2002) revisions show no public progress; clozapine post-REMS monitoring guidance is unchanged; no confirmed HEDIS FUH/FUM/FUA measurement-year-2026 specification changes were identified (NCQA posted MY2026 measure-trending determinations June 26, 2026, but the specific FUH/FUM/FUA trending designation could not be independently confirmed this pass — verify before citing).
Context, not a guideline change: SAMHSA experienced a chaotic grant termination-and-restoration episode January 13–15, 2026 (~$2B, ~2,000–2,900 grantees affected then restored) — relevant to the stability of downstream community referral partners (peer support, prevention, treatment) BHU2 relies on for discharge planning.

Practice-Changing Entries in Detail
CMS FY2027 IPF PPS/IPFQR Proposed Rule (CMS-1847-P)
Category: Updated / Quality metric / Workflow  |  Priority: NOW
Published in the Federal Register April 7, 2026; public comment period closed June 1, 2026; final rule expected on the usual early-August cadence for an October 1 FY start.
Top provisions:
Proposed 2.3% payment-rate update (2021-based market basket 3.1% less a 0.8-point productivity adjustment); ~2.1%/$50M net increase to total IPF payments.
Proposed facility-level outlier payment cap: outlier payments limited to no more than 20% of an IPF’s total IPF PPS payments in a year.
IPFQR proposes removing two measures beginning with the CY2026 reporting period/FY2028 payment determination: SUB-2/2a (Alcohol Use Brief Intervention Provided or Offered) and TOB-3/3a (Tobacco Use Treatment Provided or Offered at Discharge).
Standardized IPF Patient Assessment Instrument (IPF-PAI), per CAA-2023 §4125(b)(1): five statutory domains (functional status; cognitive function/mental status; special services, treatments, and interventions; medical conditions/comorbidities; impairments); proposed quarterly submission beginning October 1, 2027; compliance threshold of 100% of items completed for ≥80% of submitted assessments to avoid a payment reduction; submission via CMS web application or an HL7 FHIR-based API (the first CMS statutory QRP to use the FHIR standard).
Impact on adult inpatient psychiatry: The IPF-PAI is the most significant medium-term documentation-burden signal since the program’s inception — a structured, item-level patient assessment distinct from claims-based quality measures, with an EHR-interoperability (FHIR) requirement. Begin scoping EHR-vendor FHIR capability now; the 15-month runway to October 2027 is short for a system integration project.
Impact on discharge planning: SUB-2/2a and TOB-3/3a removal (if finalized) reduces discharge-documentation burden for alcohol brief-intervention and tobacco-treatment offer/provision, but does not change the clinical standard of care for either.
Impact on family involvement: No direct provision; family/caregiver-reported functional and cognitive status may become relevant IPF-PAI data sources depending on final item specifications.
Impact on documentation/EHR workflows: Confirm EHR vendor roadmap for FHIR-based IPF-PAI submission; flag SUB-2/2a and TOB-3/3a fields for removal pending the final rule; do not remove until finalized — CMS has reversed IPFQR measure decisions before (see baseline: SDOH/health-equity measures added FY2024, removed FY2026).
Source: CMS FY2027 IPF PPS Proposed Rule fact sheet; Federal Register, CMS-1847-P (Apr 7, 2026)
Auvelity (Dextromethorphan-Bupropion) — Alzheimer’s Agitation
Category: New drug / Safety  |  Priority: NOW
FDA-approved April 30, 2026 for agitation associated with dementia due to Alzheimer’s disease — an expanded indication for a drug first approved in 2022 for MDD. First-in-class NMDA/sigma-1 mechanism; the first FDA-approved treatment for AD agitation that is NOT an antipsychotic.
Top practice-changing points:
Provides a non-antipsychotic first option for AD-related agitation, sidestepping the antipsychotic-class Boxed Warning (increased mortality in elderly dementia patients) that still applies to brexpiprazole (captured in the baseline as the first-approved agent, May 2023).
Combination therapy (dextromethorphan + bupropion, marketed as Auvelity) — review for bupropion-related contraindications (seizure disorder, eating disorders) before use in the geriatric/dementia population, distinct from brexpiprazole’s risk profile.
Impact on adult inpatient psychiatry: A meaningful new option for BHU2’s dementia-with-agitation admissions, particularly where families or the treating team want to avoid antipsychotic exposure; still requires a structured, time-limited trial with reassessment akin to brexpiprazole use.
Impact on discharge planning: Document the specific agitation-management rationale (antipsychotic vs. Auvelity vs. non-pharmacologic) and hand off a reassessment plan and taper/continuation decision to the outpatient or long-term-care prescriber.
Impact on family involvement: A genuine talking point for families wary of antipsychotics in a demented relative — frame as a non-antipsychotic option with its own distinct risk profile (bupropion seizure-risk, stimulating effects), not as risk-free.
Impact on documentation/EHR workflows: Add Auvelity to the dementia-agitation order-set option list alongside brexpiprazole; require documented rationale and reassessment date for either.
Source: FDA press announcement; Psychiatric Times
Bysanti (Milsaperidone)
Category: New drug  |  Priority: PLAN
FDA-approved February 20, 2026 (Vanda Pharmaceuticals) for schizophrenia and for manic or mixed episodes of bipolar I disorder in adults. A new chemical entity, atypical antipsychotic, described as bioequivalent to iloperidone across the therapeutic dosing range. U.S. commercial availability anticipated Q3 2026.
Impact on adult inpatient psychiatry: A new antipsychotic option entering formularies mid-to-late 2026; treat as iloperidone-class for practical purposes (QTc/titration/CYP2D6 considerations typical of the class) pending BHU2 pharmacy P&T review once commercially available.
Impact on discharge planning / family involvement: Standard new-antipsychotic discharge counseling (metabolic monitoring, QTc if relevant, adherence) applies; no dementia or pediatric indication.
Impact on documentation/EHR workflows: Add to formulary-review queue; no order-set changes needed until pharmacy completes P&T review.
Source: Psychiatric Times; Vanda Pharmaceuticals press release
SAMHSA Harm-Reduction Funding Restriction (Dear Colleague Letter)
Category: Workflow / Policy  |  Priority: PLAN
Issued April 24, 2026, updating July 2025 guidance issued under the administration’s “Ending Crime and Disorder on America’s Streets” executive order. Narrows which supplies/services SAMHSA grantees may fund as harm reduction.
Excluded from federal grant funding: fentanyl test strips and sterile syringes for direct distribution to people who use drugs (test strips remain fundable for use by public-health officials, law enforcement, and medical professionals in official capacities).
Still fundable: naloxone, sharps-disposal kits, and infectious-disease (HIV/hepatitis) testing and vaccination.
A companion letter urges that methadone and buprenorphine be framed as a pathway to recovery rather than indefinite maintenance treatment — a messaging shift, not (as of this review) a change to FDA labeling or clinical practice standards for MOUD.
Impact on adult inpatient psychiatry: Does not change inpatient MOUD initiation or continuation practice directly, but affects the harm-reduction and MOUD-linkage capacity of community partners BHU2 refers to at discharge — verify current service availability with local harm-reduction/OTP partners rather than assuming continuity.
Impact on discharge planning: If a discharge plan has historically included referral to a syringe-service or test-strip-distribution program, confirm the program is still funded/operating before including it; naloxone provision at discharge is unaffected.
Impact on family involvement: None directly; may affect messaging if family asks about long-term methadone/buprenorphine — continue to counsel per clinical evidence (VA/DoD SUD guideline, ASAM) rather than the administrative framing.
Impact on documentation/EHR workflows: Review discharge-referral resource lists for harm-reduction services annually given funding volatility; no order-set changes indicated.
Source: National Association of Counties summary; NWVCIL policy brief
Joint Commission / NQF Sentinel Events List Alignment
Category: Updated / Workflow  |  Priority: PLAN
Announced January 26, 2026; effective January 1, 2027 across all accredited organizations. Joint Commission is adopting NQF’s updated Serious Reportable Events (SRE) List as part of its Sentinel Events (SE) List, ending the need to track two separate safety-event frameworks.
Three legacy workplace-safety sentinel events are explicitly retained in the consolidated list: Homicide, Sexual Abuse/Assault, and Physical Assault of a staff member.
A joint Aligning Patient Safety Event Reporting report provides a crosswalk between the SRE and SE lists to guide implementation.
Impact on adult inpatient psychiatry: Directly reinforces the existing R3 #42 (BHC workplace-violence-prevention program, eff. July 1, 2024) standard already in the baseline — staff-assault events now sit inside the same mandatory sentinel-event reporting framework as patient-safety events, raising their institutional visibility.
Impact on discharge planning / family involvement: No direct effect.
Impact on documentation/EHR workflows: Confirm BHU2’s incident-reporting system captures Homicide, Sexual Abuse/Assault, and Physical Assault of staff as sentinel-event-reportable categories ahead of the January 1, 2027 effective date; align with the existing workplace-violence annual worksite analysis (R3 #42).
Source: Joint Commission announcement; NQF announcement
