## Priority actions (do these first)

1. **[both][P1] Delirium page — name the antipsychotic *harm* signal, not just "no benefit."** The page correctly says antipsychotics are "for dangerous agitation only, never for prevention," but omits that in **palliative care both risperidone and haloperidol were *worse* than placebo, with haloperidol associated with worse survival (HR 1.73, 95% CI 1.20–2.50; Agar 2017)**, and that the large ICU trials were flatly negative (MIND-USA, AID-ICU, REDUCE). This upgrades "not helpful" to "can harm." (Delirium teaching page, *Management*.)
2. **[both][P1] Delirium page — add the discharge-continuation deprescribing risk.** ~**one-third of older adults started on antipsychotics in hospital continue them after discharge**, and discontinuation after hospitalization is associated with **lower rehospitalization and all-cause mortality** (2025 trial-emulation). The page stresses "time-limited" but not this specific, common, preventable error. (Delirium teaching page, *Management* / *What the student does*.)
3. **[Resident][P1] Clozapine (Advanced Psychopharm page) — add the myocarditis monitoring specifics.** The page says "vigilance for myocarditis (especially early)" without the protocol: **incidence ~0.7%, 83% of cases days 14–21; troponin + CRP at baseline and weekly for the first month; stop if troponin >2× ULN or CRP >100 mg/L** (Ronaldson; Segev 2021). This is the single most actionable safety gap. (`adv_psychopharmacology.md`, *Schizophrenia and clozapine*.)
4. **[both][P1] Clozapine (primer + adv page) — GI hypomotility carries a *higher* case-fatality than agranulocytosis.** The adv page says constipation "can progress to ileus and be fatal"; add the number that makes the point land: **CIGH case-fatality 15–27.5% vs. 2.2–4.2% for agranulocytosis**, self-reported constipation is only **18% sensitive** for objective hypomotility, so **prophylactic laxatives for all** and **avoid concomitant anticholinergics (OR 5.88 for fatal ileus)**. (Every-Palmer 2016/2017/2020.)
5. **[both][P1] Violence Risk page — teach the base rate and that the tools rule *out* better than they rule *in*.** Add that **~17% of acute psychiatric inpatients commit ≥1 act of violence during admission** (Iozzino 2015) and that short-term tools (BVC/DASA) have **high NPV (97–99.5%) but low PPV (<15%)** because of the low base rate. Prevents over-reading a single positive score. (Violence Risk teaching page, *Recognize and screen*.)

## A. Clozapine → Psychopharm primer + Advanced Psychopharmacology (resident) + Monitoring page
*Source: "Clozapine in Inpatient Psychiatry: Comprehensive Evidence Review." No dedicated clozapine page exists; these target the three pages that carry clozapine content. The primer and adv pages are **accurate on the Feb-2025 FDA REMS elimination** — no correction needed there.*

**Accuracy corrections**

- **[both][P1] None required on REMS status.** Both pages already state correctly that "the FDA eliminated the clozapine REMS in 2025" and that ANC monitoring continues per the prescribing information. Confirmed against the review (§7: "In February 2025, the FDA eliminated the clozapine REMS program… the manufacturer's recommendations for ANC monitoring remain in effect"). Leave as-is.

**Completeness additions (missing high-yield numbers)**

- **[both][P1] Efficacy numbers behind "the agent for TRS."** Primer says clozapine "is the agent for treatment-resistant schizophrenia… uniquely effective." Add the landmark figure (also flagged in the 2026-06-30 memo and still missing): **response ~30% vs. ~4% for chlorpromazine in rigorously defined TRS (Kane 1988; effect size −0.88), TRS defined as failure of ≥2 adequate antipsychotic trials.** One-line phrasing: "In treatment-resistant schizophrenia, ~30% respond to clozapine vs. ~4% to a comparator (Kane 1988) — the largest effect ever shown for an antipsychotic."
- **[Resident][P1] Myocarditis protocol (see Priority Action 3).** Adv page + Monitoring page: **~0.7% incidence, onset weeks 2–6 (83% days 14–21), CFR ~12.7%; troponin + CRP baseline then weekly ×4 weeks; cessation at troponin >2× ULN or CRP >100 mg/L** (Ronaldson protocol; Segev 2021).
- **[both][P1] GI hypomotility case-fatality (see Priority Action 4).** Numbers: **CFR 15–27.5% vs. 2.2–4.2% for agranulocytosis; constipation self-report 18% sensitive; prophylactic laxatives for all; anticholinergic co-prescription OR 5.88 for fatal ileus** (Every-Palmer; Cohen 2017).
- **[Resident][P2] Seizures are dose-dependent, and the AED choice matters.** Adv page says "dose-related seizure risk." Add: **~5% cumulative 1-yr incidence — 1.0% at <300 mg/day, 2.7% at 300–600, 4.4% at ≥600**; for co-prescribed anticonvulsant **prefer lamotrigine or topiramate over valproate** (valproate add-on linked to myocarditis/neutropenia) and **carbamazepine is contraindicated** (agranulocytosis + CYP induction) (Devinsky 1991; Verdoux 2024).
- **[Resident][P2] All-cause mortality is *lowest* on clozapine.** Powerful counter-frame to the "dangerous drug" reputation: **FIN20 — lowest all-cause mortality of any antipsychotic (aHR 0.39); cumulative 20-yr mortality 15.6% (clozapine) vs. 46.2% (non-users)** (Taipale 2020). Ties to the existing "anti-suicidal effect nothing else has" pearl.
- **[both][P2] Quantify the smoking/CYP1A2 interaction the adv page already describes qualitatively.** Add: **nonsmokers run plasma clozapine ~136% higher than smokers; on cessation levels rise 29–50% within days → reduce dose 30–40% with TDM** (Wagner 2020). Vaping = treat as cessation; NRT does not affect CYP1A2.
- **[Resident][P2] Duffy-null / benign ethnic neutropenia.** Consider **ACKR1 genotype testing before starting**; BEN pathway thresholds **initiate at ANC ≥1,000/μL and continue at 500–999/μL** (vs. interrupt in general population) — reduces unnecessary discontinuations in patients of African/Caribbean/Middle-Eastern/Mediterranean descent (Legge 2019; 2015 REMS BEN pathway).
- **[Resident][P2] Name the FDA anti-suicidal indication with its number.** Clozapine is the only antipsychotic FDA-indicated to reduce recurrent suicidal behavior: **InterSePT HR 0.76 vs. olanzapine (Meltzer 2003)**; register data HR 0.64–0.66 (Taipale 2021).

## B. Catatonia → `catatonia_inpatient_teaching.md`
*Source: "Comprehensive Evidence Review: Catatonia." The page is excellent and attested. One deliberate pedagogical choice to revisit, plus number-level completeness.*

**Accuracy / consistency**

- **[Resident][P2] Revisit the "do not quote a dose" instruction for resident-level content.** Current text: *"Do **not** memorize or quote a dose — follow your institutional protocol and your supervising clinician's direction for dosing and monitoring."* That is defensible for MS3, but the **2025 APA Resource Document now gives a standardized protocol (lorazepam 2 mg IV challenge; 2 mg = 4 mg with no added benefit), maintenance 8–24 mg/day**. Recommend keeping the MS3 page dose-free but adding the concrete protocol to a resident-facing page — and reconciling with the Evidence page, which (per the 2026-06-30 memo) *does* quote "2 mg IV; 8–24 mg/day." Flagging the same internal inconsistency again. *(Attestation flag.)*

**Completeness additions**

- **[both][P2] Give the screen its threshold and add the rapid screen.** Page says "use its screening items." Add: **BFCSI 14-item screen positive at ≥2; in ICU use a higher ≥4 cutoff (91% sensitivity/specificity, Wilson 2017)**, and the new **Catatonia Quick Screen (CQS) — 4 items (excitement, mutism, staring, posturing), 97% sensitivity** (Luccarelli 2025).
- **[both][P2] Put numbers on the lorazepam challenge.** Page says "meaningful, often rapid improvement." Add: **response ~77% overall, up to ~90% in acute catatonia; a negative challenge does NOT rule out catatonia (~24% don't respond)** (Bot 2026 meta-analysis; Heckers 2023). Reinforces the page's existing (correct) caution.
- **[both][P2] Numbers for ECT and malignant catatonia.** Page says ECT is for severe/malignant/refractory disease. Add: **ECT response 59–100% (76–92% in benzodiazepine-refractory); untreated malignant catatonia is fatal in 10–20%; combined benzodiazepine + ECT gives the best outcomes** (Cronemeyer 2022; APA 2025).
- **[Resident][P2] Antipsychotic risk — quantify and add the clozapine nuance.** Page correctly says "avoid antipsychotics until catatonia is excluded." Add: **pre-existing catatonia raises NMS risk to ~3.6% (vs. 0.07–1.8% baseline)**; when an antipsychotic is unavoidable in chronic schizophrenia-associated catatonia, **clozapine <150 mg/day is preferred; high-potency FGAs (haloperidol ROR 3.8) avoided** (Da Costa 2022; APA 2025).
- **[Resident][P2] NMS-vs-catatonia discriminators.** For the resident differential: **diaphoresis (OR 10), rigor (OR 9.6), fever (OR 7.3) favor NMS; negativism, posturing, waxy flexibility, stereotypy favor catatonia** — though the overlap is a continuum (Virolle 2023).

## C. Delirium → `delirium_inpatient_teaching.md`
*Source: "Delirium for the Psychiatry Resident." The page is strong and attested; the two P1 items are in Priority Actions 1–2. Completeness below.*

**Completeness additions**

- **[both][P2] Add the 4AT as a no-training bedside screen.** Page teaches the CAM only. High-yield addition: **the 4AT requires no training, takes <2 min (sens/spec ~88%)**, and critically **CAM sensitivity falls to ~40% when scored without formal cognitive testing** vs. 4AT ~76% in the same patients (MacLullich 2019). Suggested phrasing: "The CAM is the reference standard but is training-dependent; the 4AT needs no training and is the better bedside screen when formal cognitive testing isn't done."
- **[both][P2] Prevention works — give the number.** Page names the HELP/ABCDEF bundles. Add: **HELP reduces delirium incidence ~53% (OR 0.47; Hshieh 2018); ≥30–40% of delirium is preventable.** The components with individual evidence are **sleep hygiene (OR 0.25), reorientation (OR 0.32), cognitive stimulation (OR 0.45)** (Burton 2021 Cochrane NMA).
- **[Resident][P2] Dexmedetomidine is the one agent with consistent evidence.** For the ICU consult: **dexmedetomidine reduces delirium vs. other sedatives (RR 0.67; SCCM PADIS conditional recommendation)** — watch bradycardia (RR 2.39)/hypotension. No antipsychotic reduces delirium duration in adequately powered RCTs (MIND-USA/AID-ICU).
- **[both][P2] Long-term consequences.** Add one line: **delirium independently predicts incident dementia (subdistribution HR 3.0), institutionalization, and death** (Marcantonio 2017; large cohort 2025). Motivates families and the deprescribing effort.

## D. ECT & Neuromodulation → `ect_neuromodulation_inpatient_teaching.md`
*Source: "ECT & Neuromodulation: Comprehensive Evidence Review." The page is excellent and attested; the additions are number-level and a few resident-track items.*

**Completeness additions**

- **[both][P2] Psychotic depression is the single strongest indication — give the number.** Page lists psychotic features among ECT indications. Add: **CORE study — 87% overall remission with bilateral ECT, 95% in psychotic vs. 83% in nonpsychotic depression (Petrides 2001); psychotic features independently predict remission (OR 1.47)** (van Diermen 2018).
- **[both][P2] Quantify efficacy vs. sham.** Add: **UK ECT Review Group — real vs. sham effect size −0.91; pooled response 60–80%, remission 50–60%** (2003). Anchors "most effective treatment."
- **[both][P2] Put the numbers on the continuation point the page already makes.** Page says "lithium + nortriptyline reduced post-ECT relapse in the Sackeim trial." Add: **without continuation, relapse ~84% at 6 months; nortriptyline + lithium cut it to 39% vs. 60% for nortriptyline alone (Sackeim 2001)** — reinforces "never discharge post-ECT without a continuation plan."
- **[Resident][P2] ELEKT-D for the resident TRD differential.** **IV ketamine was noninferior to ECT in nonpsychotic TRD (response 55.4% vs. 41.2%), with less memory impairment (Anand, NEJM 2023)** — caveat: open-label, mostly outpatient, RUL not bilateral. (Mirrors the 2026-06-30 note for the adv page; applies to the ECT page's rapid-acting section too.)
- **[Resident][P2] iTBS and the RUL dosing caveat.** Add: **intermittent theta-burst (iTBS, ~3 min) is noninferior to standard 37.5-min 10 Hz rTMS (THREE-D, Lancet 2018)**; and refine the page's "right unilateral is gentler on memory" to note **RUL must be dosed at 5–6× seizure threshold to match bitemporal efficacy** (Sackeim 2000; Kolshus 2017).

## E. Long-Acting Injectable Antipsychotics → `14_Tracks/Resident/adv_psychopharmacology.md` (+ Psychosis page, MS3)
*Source: "LAIs in Schizophrenia, Bipolar Disorder, and Recurrent Psychiatric Hospitalization." No dedicated LAI page; the resident adv page carries one paragraph on LAIs, which is accurate but non-quantitative.*

**Accuracy corrections**

- **[Resident][P2] None — but sharpen the framing.** Current adv text: *"Consider an LAI when nonadherence drives relapse, when covert nonadherence is suspected, or to prevent the readmission cycle…"* Accurate, but the evidence now positions LAIs as an **early-phase option, not a last resort**. No factual error; recommend a framing addition (below).

**Completeness additions**

- **[Resident][P2] Effectiveness magnitude + the RCT-vs-real-world gap (the key teaching point).** Add: **real-world studies show 20–50% reductions in rehospitalization and ~20–60% in all-cause mortality; within-subject data show ~48% fewer hospitalizations (Wei 2022).** Teach *why* RCTs understate it: **NNT ~45 in RCTs vs. ~4 in mirror-image studies (Kishimoto 2021)** because RCTs select adherent patients — the exact advantage LAIs target.
- **[Resident][P2] Early / first-episode benefit — reframe from "last resort."** **PRELAPSE cluster-RCT: 44% reduction in first hospitalization (NNT 7) in early-phase schizophrenia (Kane 2020); FEP relapse 5% (LAI) vs. 33% (oral) (Subotnik 2015).** Benefit is larger in incident than prevalent cohorts (AHR 0.74 vs. 0.84).
- **[Resident][P2] Highest-yield subgroups.** **Comorbid substance use (EULAST: 36% lower discontinuation), justice-involved (PRIDE: 39.8% vs. 53.7% treatment failure), frequent prior admissions.**
- **[Resident][P2] Mortality signal.** **SGA-LAIs have the largest protective effect against all-cause mortality of any antipsychotic regimen (RR 0.39; Correll 2022); LAI vs. oral 21% lower all-cause mortality (Aymerich 2025)** — benefit concentrated in early switching (within 3 years).
- **[Resident][P2] One practical pearl.** **Paliperidone palmitate is the only LAI with a true loading strategy (no oral overlap); Risperdal Consta requires 21 days of oral overlap.** Useful at the point of ordering.
- **[Resident][P2] Equity flag worth teaching.** **Black patients are both more likely to receive LAIs overall AND disproportionately receive older FGA-LAIs (Medicare SGA-LAI 60.1% vs. 69.9%; Li 2023), and perceive more coercion** — so offer LAIs proactively and via shared decision-making, framed by the principle that "the choice is made each time an injection is administered" (PRELAPSE). *(Attestation flag — sensitive framing.)*
- **[both][P2] Psychosis page (MS3) micro-add.** The disposition paragraph mentions "adequate insight or supervision for adherence" but not LAIs. Suggested one-liner: "For patients whose relapses are driven by nonadherence, a long-acting injectable started during admission is an evidence-based option — most effective when begun early, not reserved as a last resort."
- **[Resident][P2] Bipolar caveat.** **Aripiprazole once-monthly is FDA-approved for bipolar I maintenance (relapse 26.5% vs. 51.1%; Calabrese 2017); LAIs prevent mania, not depression**, and paliperidone palmitate is **not** FDA-approved for bipolar (schizoaffective only).

## F. Violence Risk → `violence_risk_inpatient_teaching.md`
*Source: "Violence Risk Assessment and Management in Inpatient Psychiatry." Page is strong and attested; P1 base-rate/NPV item is in Priority Action 5. Completeness below.*

**Completeness additions**

- **[both][P2] Name the DASA alongside the BVC.** Page cites the BVC (correctly) and defers HCR-20 to forensic work. Add the **DASA (7 items, 24-h horizon)** because it is **the only tool with RCT evidence that structured scoring + a management protocol actually reduces aggression (eDASA+APP, OR 0.56; Griffith 2021)** and reduces PRN medication use (OR 0.64). Suggested phrasing: "The BVC and the closely related DASA are the two bedside short-term tools; pairing either with an action protocol — not just scoring — is what reduces violence."
- **[both][P2] Quantify substance use as the dominant risk factor.** Page lists substance use (static) and intoxication/withdrawal (dynamic). Add: **substance misuse is the single strongest psychiatric risk factor (pooled OR 7.4); comorbid substance use raises the schizophrenia–violence OR from 3.5 to 9.9 (Whiting 2022)** — and the operative factor is the **dynamic state of intoxication/withdrawal**, not the SUD diagnosis (inpatient assault is paradoxically *lower* in SUD patients once intoxication clears).
- **[both][P2] Anger mediates the delusion–violence link.** Page names "persecutory delusions plus command hallucinations" as highest-risk (correct). Add the nuance that **anger is the mediator: the population-attributable risk of anger from persecutory-type delusions is 55.9% for serious violence (Coid 2013)**, and delusion–violence associations shrink when measured with temporal separation. Teaching point: "Monitor anger and the patient's coping with the belief, not just the belief's content."
- **[Resident][P2] Rapid tranquilization hierarchy (adv/resident, since the MS3 page defers dosing).** **A 2026 Lancet Psychiatry IPD network meta-analysis found antipsychotic–benzodiazepine combinations among the most effective for acute agitation, while haloperidol monotherapy was among the least effective and had more EPS** (Siafis 2026). Reconcile with the Agitation page per the 2026-06-30 memo's standing "reconcile agents" flag.
