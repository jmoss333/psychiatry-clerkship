## A. Psychopharmacology Canon → Psychopharm primer (MS3) + Advanced Psychopharmacology (Resident)

*Source: "The Psychiatry Resident Psychopharmacology Canon: An Evidence-Based Compendium" (>600 sources, 13 domains). Targets `05_Psychopharmacology/Student_Primer_Top10/psychopharmacology_primer_inpatient.md` and `14_Tracks/Resident/adv_psychopharmacology.md`. Both were attested 2026-07-09 and are strong; findings below are the Canon-specific numbers **not already covered** in the 2026-06-30 or 2026-07-20 memos.*

**Accuracy corrections**

- **[Resident][P1] STAR\*D cumulative remission is overstated.** Current adv-page text (verbatim): *"STAR\*D's enduring lesson is humility — remission rates fell with each successive step and roughly a third never remitted, so neither switching nor augmenting is clearly superior; the move that helps *this* patient is empirical."* Issue: *"roughly a third never remitted"* encodes the original ~67% cumulative-remission figure. The Canon's key correction: **the ~67% figure was challenged by a 2023 reanalysis using protocol-stipulated blinded ratings, which found the actual cumulative remission ≈ 35.0% — about half the original** (Pigott et al. 2023). Suggested phrasing: *"…remission rates fell with each successive step; the often-quoted ~67% cumulative remission was overstated — a protocol-adherent 2023 reanalysis put it near 35% (i.e., roughly two-thirds never remitted). Neither switching nor augmenting is clearly superior…"* The "humility" framing is directionally right; the number is what needs fixing. *(Attestation flag.)*

**Completeness additions (missing high-yield numbers)**

- **[MS3][P2] CATIE discontinuation number (primer).** Current primer text: *"The single most useful framing comes from CATIE: choose by side-effect profile, not by 'newer is better'…"* Add the anchor: **74% of patients discontinued before 18 months (median 4.6 months); olanzapine had the longest time to discontinuation but the greatest metabolic burden; perphenazine (an FGA) was comparably effective** (Lieberman 2005). One-liner: "CATIE's punchline is a number — 74% stopped their drug within 18 months, and the old first-generation perphenazine held its own."
- **[MS3][P2] Metabolic screening is done poorly — give the number (primer).** Current text: *"metabolic monitoring is part of the prescription: baseline weight, glucose, and lipids, then ongoing tracking."* Add: **in practice baseline glucose is checked ~23% of the time and lipids ~8%** (Mitchell 2012) — makes the student's "surface what's due" job concrete.
- **[both][P2] Antipsychotics in dementia — the black-box number.** Not on the primer; high-yield for geriatric/agitation admissions: **antipsychotics raise mortality ~1.6–1.7× in elderly dementia patients (NNH ~26 for haloperidol to ~50 for quetiapine over ~180 days)** (Maust 2015; Schneider 2005). Target: primer *or* the geriatric/agitation page (verify which already carries it).
- **[Resident][P2] BALANCE numbers (adv).** Current text: *"Lithium remains the maintenance benchmark…"* Add: **lithium monotherapy superior to valproate monotherapy (HR 0.71); lithium + valproate superior to valproate alone (HR 0.59, NNT 7); valproate has no FDA maintenance approval** (BALANCE 2010).
- **[Resident][P2] Lamotrigine polarity specificity — numbers (adv).** Current text: *"Lamotrigine is the maintenance agent for the depressive pole."* Add: **lamotrigine prevents the depressive pole, lithium the manic pole (complementary; pooled median time-to-intervention lamotrigine 197 d, lithium 184 d, placebo 86 d)** (Bowden/Calabrese 2003; Goodwin 2004).
- **[Resident][P2] Antipsychotic deprescribing evidence (adv — currently absent).** Serves the page's "escalate/own the monitoring" theme and the standing deprescribe-before-discharge point: **RADAR — antipsychotic dose reduction doubled severe relapse (25% vs 13%, OR 2.20); HAMLETT — reduction meant worse short-term relapse but better long-term functioning at 3–4 yr; Wunderink 7-yr — higher functional recovery with early dose reduction (55.9% vs 21.7%)**. Teaching point: dose reduction in psychosis is a long-horizon bet with a real short-term relapse cost, not a free taper.
- **[Resident][P2] Antidepressant discontinuation numbers (adv).** **ANTLER — 56% relapse on discontinuation vs 39% on continuation (HR 2.06), but 44% who stopped did not relapse; a 2026 network meta-analysis found slow taper alone did NOT beat abrupt stopping — adding psychological support was decisive (NNT 4.3 for continuation + support)** (Lewis 2021; 2026 NMA). Pairs with the page's "verify before you escalate" ethos on the discontinuation side.
- **[Resident][P2] Clozapine ANC schedule + the 2025 Delphi (adv).** Current text names ANC monitoring "per the prescribing information." Add the schedule residents are asked about: **weekly ×6 months → biweekly months 7–12 → monthly thereafter; a 2025 global Delphi consensus recommends stopping routine ANC monitoring after 2 years and lowering cessation thresholds, because 85–90% of agranulocytosis occurs in the first year.** Complements the 2026-07-20 clozapine safety additions (myocarditis, CIGH, seizures).
- **[Resident][P2] Pharmacogenomics — a pre-emptive accuracy caution.** The adv page doesn't mention combinatorial PGx; a one-liner would pre-empt over-ordering: **GUIDED missed its primary endpoint (p=0.107); PRIME Care showed only small, nonpersistent symptom effects; the APA Council on Research (2024) concluded current combinatorial PGx tools are not supported for MDD treatment selection — the defensible use is CPIC single gene-drug guidance (CYP2D6/CYP2C19).** *(Verify no other resident page currently endorses combinatorial PGx.)*
- **[Resident][P2] Polypharmacy reassessment cadence (adv).** Anchors the de-prescribe habit: **~1 in 4 antipsychotic users are on antipsychotic polypharmacy; the ASCP 2026 Task Force reached 100% consensus that the utility of continuing any psychotropic be reassessed at minimum annually.**

## B. Legal & Ethical Foundations → Ethics & Law page (MS3) + Inpatient Systems & Med-Legal (Resident)

*Source: "Legal and Ethical Foundations of Inpatient Psychiatry: A Comprehensive Educational Review." Targets `03_Core_Topics/Ethics_Legal/ethics_law_confidentiality_inpatient_teaching.md` (MS3) and `14_Tracks/Resident/systems_medlegal.md` (Resident). Both attested 2026-07-09. The MS3 page is accurate — no factual corrections; the gaps are named case-law and tool/number completeness. The resident page deliberately avoids statute-specific numbers (Maine Title 34-B) and federal case names; the additions below are the board/PRITE-relevant anchors it currently omits.*

**Accuracy corrections**

- **[both][P1] None required.** The MS3 page's four-abilities model (*"communicate a choice, understand, appreciate, and reason"*), capacity-vs-competence distinction, Tarasoff framing, and "commitment ≠ consent to medicate" are all correct and match the review. Leave as-is.

**Completeness additions (missing high-yield facts)**

- **[MS3][P1] Name the two commitment cases + the standard of proof.** Current text: *"Emergency holds allow short-term detention for evaluation; longer commitment requires due process (hearing)."* The page names no case law, but two are frequently tested: **Addington v. Texas (1979) — the constitutionally required standard of proof for civil commitment is "clear and convincing evidence"; O'Connor v. Donaldson (1975) — a non-dangerous mentally ill person who can survive safely in the community cannot be confined.** Suggested add to the "Involuntary hospitalization" paragraph and/or High-yield pearls.
- **[MS3][P2] Name the involuntary-medication cases.** Current text: *"Overriding that refusal requires either a true emergency (imminent danger) or a separate legal/adjudicated process (e.g., a court or independent review depending on jurisdiction)."* Accurate but unnamed; add the two the frameworks derive from: **Washington v. Harper (1990) — involuntary medication of a prisoner via administrative review (dangerous + medical interest); Sell v. United States (2003) — four-part test to medicate a defendant to restore competency to stand trial (important government interest, likely efficacy, least restrictive, medical interest).**
- **[Resident][P2] Capacity tools + the miss rate (systems_medlegal).** Current text: *"Capacity assessments are documented by the four abilities (understand, appreciate, reason, express a choice) tied to the specific decision."* Add: **MacCAT-T is the best-validated capacity instrument (inter-rater ICC 0.92–0.98); the ACE (Aid to Capacity Evaluation) is the best-supported tool for medical patients (LR+ 8.5, LR− 0.21, <30 min); without a structured tool, clinicians recognize incapacity in only ~42% of affected patients** (Sessums 2011 JAMA). Motivates the Decisional Capacity tool the page already links.
- **[Resident][P2] Capacity prevalence anchors (systems_medlegal).** Add the numbers that make "diagnosis alone never settles it" concrete: **median incapacity ~29% among psychiatric inpatients; up to ~97% in acute mania vs ~4% in personality disorder; ~39% of *voluntary* patients may lack capacity** (Owen 2008). The last figure is the teaching point — voluntary status ≠ capacity.
- **[Resident][P2] Psychiatric advance directives — give the number (systems_medlegal).** The page centers the least-restrictive principle but doesn't mention PADs. Add: **peer-facilitated PADs reduced compulsory admission from 39.9% to 27.0% (DAiP RCT, risk difference −0.13, p=0.007); a meta-analysis found advance statements the only intervention with a significant pooled effect on reducing compulsory admission (RR 0.77, 95% CI 0.60–0.98)** (Tinland 2022; de Jong 2016).
- **[Resident][P2] Procedural justice is the lever on perceived coercion (systems_medlegal).** Add: **perceived coercion tracks with involvement in decision-making and perceived fairness, not legal status per se; legally voluntary patients who feel coerced can do worse than involuntary ones (EUNOMIA); staff training to reduce restraint (RR 0.74) and shared decision-making (RR 0.75) have the strongest evidence for reducing coercive practice** (Barbui 2021). Reinforces the page's least-restrictive ethic with the "how."
