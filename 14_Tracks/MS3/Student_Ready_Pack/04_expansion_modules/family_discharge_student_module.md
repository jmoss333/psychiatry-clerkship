# Family, Discharge, And Systems Module

Generated: 2026-06-27

Audience: MS3 students.

Purpose: translate family systems, internal manuals, ED psychiatry, and discharge
materials into plain clerkship-level skills.

## Core Idea

Inpatient psychiatry does not end at symptom reduction. A patient leaves into a
system: family, housing, medications, transportation, work/school, finances,
follow-up, stigma, and safety. If the system cannot hold the plan, the plan is
not done.

## Why It Matters — The Numbers

- **The window is highest right after discharge.** Suicide risk in the first week post-discharge is on the order of ~2,950 per 100,000 person-years — with days 0–3 the highest — roughly 200× the general-population rate (Chung et al., *BMJ Open* 2019; the overall post-discharge rate of 484 per 100,000 person-years is from Chung et al., *JAMA Psychiatry* 2017). The realism of the plan matters *most* in exactly the days after the patient leaves.
- **Follow-up is often the weak link.** About 12–15% of psychiatric patients are readmitted within 30 days (20–25% for schizophrenia), and only ~50% receive a mental-health visit within 7 days. A *scheduled* appointment (not "they'll call to arrange one") is itself the intervention — pre-discharge 7-day follow-up is associated with substantially lower suicide risk.
- **Means safety is a lethality intervention, not a formality.** Firearms are involved in ~50% of US suicides and are lethal in 85–90% of attempts; structured lethal-means counseling matters: in a large quasi-experimental cohort (Boggs et al. 2020), patients with documented lethal-means assessment had fewer suicide attempts and deaths at 180 days (0.83% vs 3.3%) — an association, not a randomized effect. This is why the "who can confirm items are secured?" phrase below is a clinical act.
- **Bringing the family in changes outcomes.** Family psychoeducation lowers relapse from ~37% to ~10% (OR 0.18), and family involvement during admission nearly triples the odds of 7-day follow-up (Haselden 2019).

*Numbers are evidence-anchored and reviewed and attested by Joshua Moss, MD (2026-07-09); use them to explain the "why," not to quote at families.*

## What Students Should Notice

Family/support context:

- Who noticed the change?
- Who brought the patient in?
- Who is trusted?
- Who is feared?
- Who minimizes risk?
- Who escalates conflict?
- Who can help with medication, sleep, appointments, transportation, means safety?

Discharge context:

- Where will the patient sleep?
- Who will be there?
- What follow-up is scheduled?
- What medication access exists?
- What transportation exists?
- What warning signs should trigger help?
- What crisis plan is realistic?

## Family Meeting Agenda Template

1. Purpose of meeting.
2. Patient voice: what the patient wants discussed.
3. Current clinical status in plain language.
4. Safety and warning signs.
5. Medication and treatment plan.
6. Family/support roles and limits.
7. Discharge barriers.
8. Follow-up and crisis plan.
9. What remains uncertain.
10. Next steps and who owns each task.

## Student Role In A Family Meeting

Before:

- Ask the team what your role is.
- Review the patient consent/permission plan.
- Write 2-3 questions you may ask.
- Know what not to promise.

During:

- Listen for emotion and mismatch.
- Track concrete tasks.
- Use plain language.
- Do not take sides.
- Ask permission before sensitive topics.

After:

- Summarize what changed.
- Identify unresolved barriers.
- Document only according to local policy.
- Debrief with supervisor.

## Useful Student Phrases

Validation:

> "It makes sense that everyone is exhausted and scared."

Clarifying role:

> "Our goal is not to make the family responsible for treatment. Our goal is to
> understand what support is realistic and what would be unsafe to assume."

Warning signs:

> "What did you notice before hospitalization that told you something was changing?"

Means safety:

> "Who can confirm that medications/firearms/other high-risk items are secured
> before discharge?"

Discharge realism:

> "What part of the plan feels hardest to actually do at home?"

## Family Education Handout Use

Before giving a handout:

- Ask what the patient/family already understands.
- Ask permission to share written material.
- Choose one relevant handout, not a packet dump.

When giving it:

- Say why this handout fits.
- Highlight one section.
- Invite disagreement or questions.

After:

- Ask what felt useful or not.
- Document education only according to local policy.
- Do not substitute a handout for a conversation.

## Common Failure Modes

- Family is supportive but overwhelmed.
- Family agrees in meeting but cannot do the plan.
- Patient consents to collateral but later feels exposed.
- Team assumes housing is stable because an address exists.
- Discharge plan depends on a follow-up appointment that is not actually scheduled.
- Safety plan exists but means safety is unconfirmed.
- Patient improves on unit but sleep plan at home is unrealistic.

## Further Reading & Evidence-Based Resources

### Discharge Planning & Safety Frameworks

**Post-Discharge Suicide Risk: Landmark Evidence**
- Chung et al. (2017). *JAMA Psychiatry*. Overall post-discharge suicide rate ~484 per 100,000 person-years; 30-day readmission risk ~12–15% (20–25% for schizophrenia). Underscores urgency of scheduled 7-day follow-up.
- Chung et al. (2019). *BMJ Open*. Days 0–3 post-discharge carry highest risk (~2,950 per 100,000 person-years, ~200× baseline). Safety planning and means reduction interventions are highest-yield in this window.

**Lethal Means Assessment & Counseling**
- Boggs et al. (2020). Quasi-experimental cohort: structured lethal-means assessment associated with fewer suicide attempts and deaths at 180 days (0.83% vs 3.3%). Firearms involved in ~50% of US suicides and lethal in 85–90% of attempts.
- Means safety during discharge is a *lethality intervention*, not a checklist item — confirm who secures high-risk items before the patient leaves.

**Family Involvement & Outcomes**
- Family psychoeducation during admission lowers relapse from ~37% to ~10% (OR 0.18) and nearly triples odds of 7-day mental-health follow-up (Haselden 2019).
- Coordinated Specialty Care (CSC) for first-episode psychosis integrates family engagement as a core component; demonstrated durable benefits on relapse and functional recovery.

### Discharge Planning Tools & Templates

**Safety Planning Intervention (SPI) Framework**
- Stanley & Brown (2012). "A collaborative framework for suicide prevention": structured safety-planning tool with collaborative completion, six key steps (warning signs, internal coping strategies, people/social settings, professional contacts, family/friends to ask for help, making the environment safer).
- Key principle: patient co-creates the plan (not clinician-written); readability and accessibility for crisis moments are essential.

**Discharge Checklist Domains**
- Safety (means, warning signs): Who can confirm items are secured? What triggers immediate help-seeking?
- Medication access: Prescription written? Pharmacy confirmed? Refill plan clear?
- Follow-up scheduling: Appointment in writing before discharge, not "they'll call to arrange."
- Transportation & housing: Confirmed address? Transportation to appointments realistic?
- Family/support roles: Clarified limits — supports are not proxies for treatment.

### Practice Guidance

**Before Discharge Planning:**
1. Map the patient's real discharge context: who lives with them? Who can help? What barriers exist?
2. Clarify your role: support mobilization, not family responsibility replacement.
3. Schedule concrete follow-up *before* discharge, not "they'll arrange it."

**During Family Meeting:**
1. Validate emotion and exhaustion.
2. Listen for assumptions (housing stable because an address exists; follow-up will happen because team says it should).
3. Identify one unresolved barrier and make a plan to address it before or immediately after discharge.

**At Discharge:**
1. Confirm means safety with a named person (medications, firearms, other high-risk items).
2. Give crisis plan in writing: include warning signs, key contact, what to do if symptoms return.
3. Hand-off to primary care and mental-health providers with clear 7-day follow-up appointment (not referral).

**After Discharge (Follow-up Coordination):**
- Coordinated hand-off to outpatient team ideally includes a brief phone call between inpatient and outpatient providers.
- Document barriers encountered and solutions for the outpatient team.
- Track 7-day and 30-day follow-up completion as a quality metric.
