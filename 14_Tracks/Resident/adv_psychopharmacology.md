# Advanced Psychopharmacology on the Inpatient Unit
> **Review status:** Reviewed and attested by Joshua Moss, MD (2026-07-09).

**In one line** — On a 4-week adult inpatient rotation, your job is not to know every drug but to sequence therapy rationally, recognize when first-line trials have genuinely failed, escalate to definitive treatments without unnecessary delay, and own the safety monitoring that keeps patients alive between doses.

**Treatment-resistant depression** — "Resistance" is a measurement claim, so make it measurable: confirm adequate dose *and* adequate duration of prior trials, verify adherence, and track response with a repeated scale (PHQ-9 or QIDS) rather than gestalt. STAR*D's enduring lesson is humility — remission rates fell with each successive step and roughly a third never remitted, so neither switching nor augmenting is clearly superior; the move that helps *this* patient is empirical. Augmentation options with the strongest support include lithium, a second-generation antipsychotic (aripiprazole is the prototypical adjunct), and T3; esketamine (intranasal, REMS-restricted) and IV ketamine offer rapid effect in TRD including with acute suicidality. Do not let an algorithm delay ECT when the clinical picture demands it: severe, psychotic, or catatonic depression, food/fluid refusal, pregnancy where pharmacology is constrained, and high acute suicide risk are all settings where ECT is reasonably *first*-line, not a last resort.

**MAOIs** — They retain a niche in atypical and genuinely refractory depression, and an inpatient stay is often the safest place to start one because you control diet and timing. Counsel on tyramine restriction (aged cheeses, cured/fermented meats, tap/draft beer, soy/fermented products) to prevent a hypertensive crisis presenting as thunderclap headache and severe hypertension. Respect washout windows in both directions — a serotonergic washout before starting an MAOI (longer for fluoxetine given its long-lived metabolite) and a washout after stopping one before reintroducing serotonergic agents — to avoid serotonin syndrome. Treat the combination of MAOI plus serotonergic or sympathomimetic agents (including many OTC cold remedies and meperidine) as contraindicated.

**Bipolar maintenance** — Lithium remains the maintenance benchmark and carries a distinct anti-suicidal signal not matched by alternatives; the trade-off is a narrow therapeutic index and real organ stewardship — monitor renal function, TSH, and calcium/PTH, and remember that NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, and thiazides raise levels toward toxicity. Valproate is effective, particularly for mixed/rapid-cycling presentations, but is teratogenic (neural tube defects) and associated with PCOS and weight gain — generally avoid in people who can become pregnant when alternatives exist. Lamotrigine is the maintenance agent for the depressive pole; its value is gated by a slow titration to mitigate Stevens-Johnson syndrome risk, and dosing must be adjusted when combined with valproate or estrogen-containing contraceptives.

**Schizophrenia and clozapine** — Among non-clozapine antipsychotics, efficacy is broadly comparable, so select by side-effect liability: weigh metabolic burden, EPS/akathisia and tardive risk, prolactin elevation, sedation, and QTc against the individual patient. The pivotal decision is recognizing treatment-resistant schizophrenia — failure of two adequate antipsychotic trials — because clozapine is the *only* agent with demonstrated superiority in TRS and also carries an anti-suicidal indication; it is routinely under-used and started too late. Clozapine's practicalities define its use: ANC monitoring for agranulocytosis (recommended per the prescribing information — the FDA discontinued the clozapine REMS in 2025, so ANC monitoring is guideline/PI-recommended, not REMS-enforced), vigilance for myocarditis (especially early), dose-related seizure risk, and severe constipation that can progress to ileus and be fatal (treat bowel regimens as non-optional). Add metabolic monitoring, manage sialorrhea, and remember the CYP1A2 interaction — smoking *induces* clearance, so abrupt smoking cessation (common at admission) can drive levels up and require dose reduction.

**Long-acting injectables and neuromodulation** — Consider an LAI when nonadherence drives relapse, when covert nonadherence is suspected, or to prevent the readmission cycle; conceptually, several second-generation agents (and a depot first-generation option) are available across multiple dosing intervals, and the inpatient stay is a natural place to initiate and bridge. For neuromodulation beyond ECT: ECT also serves as effective continuation/maintenance therapy after acute response, and rTMS is an evidence-based, non-anesthetic option for treatment-resistant *unipolar* depression in patients who can attend a daily outpatient course.

**Cross-cutting safety** — Distinguish NMS (idiosyncratic, days-long onset, lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia, markedly elevated CK) from serotonin syndrome (rapid onset, *hyperreflexia* and clonus, often GI prominence) — the tempo and the neuromuscular exam separate them. Audit cumulative QTc burden when stacking agents, monitor metabolic parameters on a defined schedule for antipsychotics, hold a low threshold for suspecting lithium toxicity during any volume or interacting-drug change, and keep the high-yield CYP interactions in view (1A2 with clozapine/smoking; fluoxetine/paroxetine as 2D6 inhibitors). Peripartum: no decision is risk-free — weigh treated illness against medication exposure collaboratively, favor agents with the most reproductive-safety data, and loop in maternal-fetal and pharmacy expertise rather than defaulting to discontinuation.

**What the resident does**
- Quantify prior trials (dose, duration, adherence) before labeling depression treatment-resistant; track response with a repeated standardized scale.
- Name the failure threshold for schizophrenia (two adequate trials) and proactively raise clozapine rather than cycling further agents.
- Identify the patient for whom ECT is first-line and advocate for it without algorithm-driven delay.
- Build the monitoring scaffolding at order entry — lithium levels and renal/thyroid/PTH, clozapine ANC and bowel regimen, antipsychotic metabolic panel, QTc on stacking.
- Reconcile interaction risks at admission, especially smoking-cessation effects on clozapine and serotonergic washouts around MAOIs.

**High-yield pearls**
- Lithium and clozapine share a distinction nothing else has: a real anti-suicidal effect — let that weight your risk-benefit math.
- Clozapine-associated constipation is a lethal, under-recognized complication; a bowel regimen is part of the prescription, not an afterthought.
- Smoking cessation raises clozapine levels (CYP1A2) — the patient who quits at admission may need a dose *decrease*, not the reflexive increase.
- Tempo plus reflexes triages the hyperthermic patient: hyporeflexic and slow-onset points to NMS; hyperreflexic with clonus points to serotonin syndrome.
- Lamotrigine's benefit lives on the depressive pole and is bought with a slow titration; rushing it buys rash.
- "Failed an antidepressant" usually means underdosed or under-duration — verify before you escalate.
