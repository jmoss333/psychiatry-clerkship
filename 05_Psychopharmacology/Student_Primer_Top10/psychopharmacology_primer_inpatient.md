# Psychopharmacology Primer — Inpatient Essentials

**In one line.** Know what the medication is meant to change, why this patient might choose it, what harm to watch for, and how the team will know it is helping.

This adult inpatient primer supports supervised learning. It provides no starting doses, titration schedules, or order sets. Use the current product label, pharmacist, supervising clinician, and local protocol for prescribing. Cases below are fictional; no patient information is used.

## Start here: five questions before choosing a drug

1. **What are we treating?** Name the diagnosis, illness phase, and target symptom. Psychosis, mania, bipolar depression, unipolar depression, withdrawal, and delirium call for different plans. Sedation alone does not show that the underlying illness is improving.
2. **What has already happened?** Ask about previous benefit, adverse effects, adequate trials, missed doses, access, and the patient's explanation of the problem.
3. **What could change the choice?** Review kidney/liver function, other medical illness, reproductive goals and pregnancy potential, overdose risk, substance use, interactions, and patient priorities.
4. **What will we measure?** Record a target such as sleep need, hallucination distress, ability to eat, or participation in care, alongside adverse effects and necessary labs.
5. **What happens after discharge?** Confirm supply, affordability, follow-up, monitoring responsibility, and a plan for missed doses or problems.

**A useful rounds sentence:** “We are treating ___ with ___ because ___. Since yesterday, ___ changed. The main adverse effect or monitoring issue is ___. The patient wants ___.”

## Antidepressants: match the burden to the person

SSRIs and SNRIs are common options for unipolar depression and several anxiety disorders. They are not immediate tranquilizers. Track early tolerability and safety while allowing an adequate supervised trial; do not label a drug ineffective after a few inpatient days. Medication, psychotherapy, sleep, activity, and social support belong in the same treatment plan. Choose together, then agree on what to reassess.

| Common examples | Why the team might choose one | What to ask about or watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SSRIs:** sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine | Familiar depression/anxiety options; prior response and interactions guide selection. | GI upset, sexual dysfunction, activation, bleeding risk with relevant co-medications, and hyponatremia in susceptible patients. Fluoxetine persists longer after stopping; citalopram has specific QT precautions. |
| **SNRIs:** [venlafaxine](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c848a5d8-ba94-4c84-80e3-0bf35fb8e32e), [duloxetine](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2f7d4d67-10c1-4bf4-a7f2-c185fbad64ba) | Depression/anxiety; duloxetine also has pain indications. | Blood pressure, sexual effects, and discontinuation symptoms. Check duloxetine's hepatic/renal restrictions. Venlafaxine missed doses can cause prominent withdrawal symptoms. |
| **Bupropion** | A different adverse-effect profile when sexual effects are a concern; norepinephrine/dopamine reuptake effects differ from SSRIs. | Activation, insomnia, seizure risk. Contraindicated with seizure disorder, current/prior bulimia or anorexia nervosa, and abrupt withdrawal from alcohol or certain sedatives. |
| **[Mirtazapine](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=98ad1917-a094-44f5-a28f-a64a8cfcd887)** | Depression when poor appetite and disturbed sleep are also important. | Sedation and weight gain may be useful to one patient and unacceptable to another. Sedation does not establish antidepressant response. |
| **[Trazodone](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fa7afa93-b98c-4dc7-bbb8-ab6a058427b9)** | An antidepressant also used off-label for sleep. | Orthostasis, next-day sedation, falls, rare priapism. A sleep-only regimen is not automatically an adequate antidepressant trial. |
| **TCAs / MAOIs:** nortriptyline, phenelzine | Specialist options in selected patients after reviewing earlier treatment. | TCAs: anticholinergic burden, cardiac effects and overdose toxicity. MAOIs: major drug/food interactions and agent-specific switching intervals. Involve pharmacy. |

**Safety anchor:** Don't start an SSRI without screening for bipolarity, and expect delayed onset. The suicidality black-box covers antidepressants through age 24 — monitor young adults closely early. Monitor clinical worsening and suicidal thinking in all ages, especially after initiation or changes; the warning calls for follow-up, not blanket withholding. Review the [sertraline label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fda754f6-d0f3-4dce-a17a-927d64f912f7) and [bupropion contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a435da9d-f6e8-4ddc-897d-8cd2bf777b21).

**Resident nuance — withdrawal versus relapse.** New dizziness, nausea, sensory symptoms, or disequilibrium soon after missed doses or a reduction should prompt a discontinuation assessment. Timing and symptom pattern help but do not settle the diagnosis. Plan an individualized taper with the prescriber rather than stopping abruptly or applying one schedule to everyone.

## Antipsychotics: compare efficacy, adverse effects, and formulation

Most familiar antipsychotics block dopamine D2 receptors; aripiprazole and cariprazine are partial agonists. Receptor actions help explain effects but do not by themselves rank benefit. Compare prior response, indication, metabolic and movement risks, prolactin, QT effects, sedation, and patient priorities.

**What CATIE actually teaches:** Choose antipsychotics by side-effect profile (CATIE), not by recency — 74% stopped their drug within 18 months. That endpoint combined lack of benefit, adverse effects, and other reasons; it did not establish equal symptom efficacy. Olanzapine had longer treatment persistence than some comparators but greater metabolic burden[^lieberman-2005-catie]. In a later network meta-analysis of acute, multi-episode schizophrenia, symptom-reduction estimates favored antipsychotics over placebo, but six drugs did not reach statistical significance; these findings should not be generalized to every clinical setting[^huhn-2019-antipsychotics].

| Familiar options | Tradeoff to recognize | Practical observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Haloperidol](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a0751f2e-05ab-4ba9-9bae-7b791bf1a621)** | Prominent dystonia, parkinsonism, akathisia and prolactin risks; QT risk depends on patient, route and exposure. | Ask about an inner need to move, stiffness, swallowing difficulty, and painful muscle contractions. |
| **[Risperidone](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=7e117c7e-02fc-4343-92a1-230061dfc5e0) / paliperidone** | Prolactin elevation, movement effects and metabolic effects; oral and long-acting products exist. | Ask about sexual/reproductive symptoms and stiffness; review renal function and the exact product. |
| **Olanzapine** | Substantial weight/metabolic burden; sedation and anticholinergic effects. | Track appetite, weight, glucose/lipids, alertness and bowel function. |
| **Quetiapine** | Sedation, orthostasis and metabolic effects; indications include selected bipolar phases. | Observe standing dizziness and daytime function; avoid a reflex sleep prescription without a clear indication. |
| **[Aripiprazole](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c040bd1d-45b7-49f2-93ea-aed7220b30ac) / cariprazine** | Often less metabolic burden than olanzapine, but akathisia and activation can be troublesome. | Ask whether “anxiety” followed a treatment change. Lower metabolic risk does not remove monitoring. |
| **[Lurasidone](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=afad3051-9df2-4c54-9684-e8262a133af8) / [ziprasidone](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5d576819-cd14-4a61-8a2f-6b90d8eac07e)** | Food requirements affect exposure; indications and QT profiles differ. | Confirm the meal requirement in the specific label. Ziprasidone requires particular attention to QT risk and interactions. |

**Older adults:** antipsychotics carry a boxed warning for increased mortality in older patients with dementia-related psychosis. Review indication and alternatives with the supervising clinician; see the [clozapine boxed warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=90876802-0e3a-44c9-9ff7-1754dfbe736a).

**Long-acting injections (LAIs):** discuss convenience, preference, and difficulty taking daily medication. An injection is not a punishment or a guarantee against relapse. The exact formulation determines tolerability testing, overlap/loading, missed-dose instructions, and visit timing. Verify these before discharge. APA guidance supports offering an LAI when the patient prefers it or adherence has been poor or uncertain[^apa-2020-schizophrenia].

**Clozapine deserves its own conversation.** Consider it for treatment-resistant schizophrenia after establishing adequate prior trials, exposure/adherence, and diagnosis. Although the FDA eliminated the clozapine REMS in 2025, **ANC monitoring remains recommended according to prescribing information**[^clozapine-rems]. The administrative change did not remove severe neutropenia risk. Ask about bowel movements, fever/infection, chest symptoms, dizziness and sedation. Missed treatment can require re-titration: never assume the previous regimen can simply resume. See the [FDA notice](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-removes-risk-evaluation-and-mitigation-strategy-rems-program-antipsychotic-drug-clozapine) and [clozapine label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=90876802-0e3a-44c9-9ff7-1754dfbe736a).

**Resident nuance — exposure can change without a prescription change.** Combustible tobacco smoke, rather than nicotine itself, induces metabolism of clozapine and olanzapine. Admission to a smoke-free setting and resumption after discharge can change exposure. Ask about smoking changes, acute illness, interactions and missed doses; involve pharmacy in the monitoring plan.

## Bipolar treatment: the phase comes first

“Mood stabilizer” does not mean a drug treats every phase. CANMAT/ISBD separates acute mania, bipolar depression, and prevention of recurrence. For bipolar I depression, antidepressant monotherapy is not recommended[^keramatian-2023-canmat-bipolar].

| Medication | Role to understand | Safety and follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Lithium](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f7f5b69a-c2a1-4586-a189-1475d41387c0)** | Acute mania and maintenance; selected bipolar-depression plans. | Narrow therapeutic window. Check kidney/thyroid function, calcium, pregnancy considerations and timed levels. Dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors/ARBs and thiazides can raise toxicity risk. |
| **[Valproate / divalproex](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=08a65cf4-7749-4ceb-6895-8f4805e2b01f)** | Acute mania and selected maintenance plans. | Review liver function, CBC/platelets, interactions and levels when indicated. New abdominal pain, vomiting or unexplained mental-status change needs assessment. Major fetal/developmental risks require specialist reproductive counseling; avoid initiation when safer effective alternatives are available. |
| **Lamotrigine** | Maintenance, especially depressive recurrence; also a guideline-supported bipolar-depression option. **Not acute antimanic treatment.** | Slow titration and missed-dose restart rules reduce serious rash risk. Valproate and estrogen-containing products alter exposure. A new rash needs prompt assessment, particularly with fever or mucosal symptoms. |
| **Selected antipsychotics** | Some treat mania, some bipolar depression, and some maintenance; indications are not interchangeable. | Quetiapine, lurasidone and cariprazine are familiar bipolar-depression options. Aripiprazole's mania/maintenance role does not establish bipolar-depression monotherapy efficacy. Apply each agent's monitoring plan. |

**Label versus guideline:** the US lamotrigine label establishes bipolar I maintenance; acute mood-episode efficacy is not established in that label. The guideline's bipolar-depression recommendation is a separate evidence judgment. Check the [lamotrigine label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d7e3572d-56fe-4727-2bb4-013ccca22678) rather than translating a recommendation into an FDA indication.

**Resident nuance — lithium and suicide outcomes.** Lithium remains an important bipolar treatment. Do not teach suicide prevention as certain: the VA randomized trial found no reduction in repeated suicide-related events with lithium added to usual care[^katz-2022-lithium-va]. A newer randomized-trial meta-analysis reported a nonsignificant reduction in suicide attempts[^wang-2025-lithium-suicidality]. These findings do not prove absence of benefit, but require more care than calling lithium the “anti-suicidal anchor.” Medication does not replace assessment, safety planning and appropriate observation.

## Anxiety, sleep, and PRNs: name the target before the order

First check pain, withdrawal, delirium, akathisia, mania, environmental disruption and unmet needs. “Anxious” and “not sleeping” are observations that need an explanation.

| Option | Useful distinction | Avoid the common trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lorazepam and other benzodiazepines** | Rapid GABA-mediated effects; important in alcohol/sedative withdrawal and catatonia, and selected short-term indications. | Scheduled treatment can be appropriate. Specify indication, reassessment and continuation/taper plan; monitor sedation, breathing and falls, especially with opioids or other sedatives. |
| **[Hydroxyzine](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=58d99cf6-2319-410b-b8be-de9c304541b6)** | Sometimes used for short-term anxiety; a sedating antihistamine. | “Non-benzodiazepine” does not mean harmless: anticholinergic effects, sedation and QT cautions matter. |
| **[Buspirone](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fa4d5cac-f687-4440-99d2-8bb27cf2f4ed)** | Scheduled treatment for selected anxiety presentations; benefit develops over time. | It is not a rapid rescue PRN. |
| **Sleep-focused measures / melatonin** | Protect sleep opportunity, reduce overnight disruption and address the cause of insomnia. | Avoid accumulating sedatives without reviewing overnight response and next-day function. |
| **Propranolol / benztropine** | May address particular medication-induced movement symptoms after assessment. | Akathisia, parkinsonism, dystonia and tardive dyskinesia are different. Benztropine is not universal “EPS” treatment and can worsen cognition/constipation. |

**Two rules can both be true:** reduce unnecessary benzodiazepine exposure. Do not abruptly stop treatment in someone likely to be physically dependent and at risk of withdrawal[^brunner-2025-benzodiazepine-taper]. Withdrawal can be severe; use an individualized supervised taper. Catatonia guidance includes benzodiazepines and ECT as central treatments; a blanket ban on scheduled benzodiazepines would misteach that care[^bap-catatonia-2023].

## Treat substance use disorders during the admission

Withdrawal treatment and longer-term addiction treatment are different jobs. Ask what the patient wants help with and arrange continuity before discharge.

| Treatment | What the learner should recognize |
| --- | --- |
| **Buprenorphine / methadone for opioid use disorder** | Evidence-based ongoing treatments. Verify current treatment, last opioid exposure and the induction/continuation plan; buprenorphine initiation can precipitate withdrawal. Sedation, interactions and methadone's QT risk need attention. Withdrawal management alone is not an adequate OUD plan. |
| **[Naltrexone](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=db911559-26f5-43a0-b61f-3425176c936f)** | An opioid antagonist used for alcohol or opioid use disorder. Confirm absence of current opioid use/dependence and the required opioid-free interval before initiation; discuss blocked opioid analgesia and hepatic precautions. |
| **[Acamprosate](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1cfe4675-766f-4f1a-b1a8-96bbdacfcfa5)** | Supports alcohol abstinence after withdrawal; assess kidney function, renal restrictions and the practicality of its schedule. |
| **Nicotine replacement / varenicline** | Offer treatment for tobacco dependence and cravings. Separately review smoke-related drug interactions; nicotine replacement does not reproduce tobacco smoke's enzyme induction. |
| **Naloxone** | Overdose rescue education and access belong in an OUD discharge plan; naloxone does not replace maintenance treatment. |

Oral naltrexone and acamprosate have evidence for reducing return to any drinking[^mcpheeters-2023-aud]. Pair medication treatment with psychosocial care. Use [SAMHSA TIP 63](https://library.samhsa.gov/product/tip-63-medications-opioid-use-disorder/pep21-02-01-002) for OUD principles and the Library's [Substance Use & Withdrawal](?page=t_sud.md) and [withdrawal assessment tool](?tool=withdrawal.html) for further learning.

## ECT, TMS, and ketamine/esketamine

| Treatment | Where it fits | What to explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ECT** | Consider early in severe depression with psychosis, catatonia, urgent medical compromise or need for rapid response, as well as selected treatment-resistant illness. | Requires anesthesia and individualized medical assessment. Discuss confusion and memory effects, including possible persistent autobiographical gaps, and plan continuation treatment. |
| **Repetitive TMS** | Noninvasive treatment for selected depressive illness, often after inadequate response or tolerability problems. | Requires a course and attendance; assess device-specific contraindications and seizure risk. It is not interchangeable with ECT when urgent stabilization is needed. |
| **IV ketamine / intranasal esketamine** | Specialist treatments with different formulations, evidence and regulatory status. IV ketamine for depression is off-label. | Discuss dissociation, sedation, blood-pressure effects and misuse risk. Rapid symptom change does not establish durable recovery or suicide prevention. |

The depression guideline informs the ECT/TMS comparison[^lam-2024-canmat-mdd]. The current US **Spravato** label permits adult treatment-resistant depression as monotherapy or with an oral antidepressant. Its indication for depressive symptoms in MDD with acute suicidal ideation/behavior requires an oral antidepressant. The label does **not** establish suicide prevention or reduction of suicidal ideation/behavior. Administration requires the REMS setting and post-treatment monitoring. See the [Spravato label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d81a6a79-a74a-44b7-822c-0dfa3036eaed) and [ECT & Neuromodulation](?page=ect_neuromodulation.md).

## Monitoring: know the purpose, result, and next owner

| Treatment | Baseline and follow-up priorities |
| --- | --- |
| **Antipsychotics** | Weight/BMI, blood pressure, glucose or A1c, lipids, movement symptoms and relevant sexual/prolactin symptoms. ECG/electrolytes according to agent and patient risk. A routine chemistry panel does not substitute for glucose/A1c and lipid assessment. |
| **Lithium** | Renal/thyroid function, calcium, hydration/interactions, pregnancy considerations and timed serum levels after relevant changes. Interpret levels with the examination and time since the last dose. |
| **Valproate** | Liver function, CBC/platelets, reproductive counseling and levels when indicated. Consider ammonia with unexplained encephalopathy; routine liver results do not exclude hyperammonemia. |
| **Clozapine** | ANC per label, bowel function, orthostasis, metabolic effects and surveillance for myocarditis/other toxicity under the local protocol. Reconcile smoking and interruptions. |
| **Antidepressants / sedatives** | Mood activation, suicidal thinking, adverse effects and interactions. Assess sodium, blood pressure, ECG, breathing or falls according to drug and patient risk. |

Check what is available, identify what is missing, and ask who will act on the result. Do not delay urgent treatment by mechanically demanding every test first; escalate to the supervising clinician. Use [Medication Monitoring & Labs](?page=med_monitoring.md) and the [Protocol Library](?page=protocol_library.md) for operational details.

## Medication emergencies: recognize and escalate

- *Serotonin syndrome:* clonus, hyperreflexia, and autonomic instability with rapid onset, usually after a serotonergic agent was started or increased[^boyer-shannon-2005-serotonin-syndrome].
- *Neuroleptic malignant syndrome:* "lead-pipe" rigidity, hyperthermia, and elevated CK in someone on a dopamine blocker — slower and stiffer than serotonin syndrome. These are typical patterns, not requirements; atypical or incomplete presentations occur.
- *Lithium toxicity:* tremor, ataxia, and confusion, often when something changed (dehydration, renal function, an interacting drug).
- *Clozapine red flags:* agranulocytosis / low ANC, myocarditis, ileus, and lowered seizure threshold — any of these is a "tell someone now" finding.
- **Also escalate:** syncope/palpitations with QT risk; excessive sedation or slowed breathing; severe rash or mucosal symptoms; airway dystonia; delirium with cumulative anticholinergic exposure.

Assess immediate safety and vital signs and contact the resident/attending or emergency response team as appropriate. Do not wait for a textbook pattern or confirmatory lab.

**Switching caution:** the familiar MAOI teaching rule is to allow ≥2 weeks after stopping most SSRIs (≥5 weeks after fluoxetine, due to its long half-life) before starting an MAOI, and ≥2 weeks after stopping an MAOI before starting a serotonergic agent[^fda-prozac-label-maoi-switching]. This is not a universal switching schedule: verify the exact pair, formulation, context and current labels with pharmacy.

## What changed overnight? Five short cases

1. **“More anxious” after an antipsychotic increase.** The patient cannot sit still and describes an internal urge to move. Ask about onset and examine for akathisia before assuming worsening psychosis or escalating the same drug. Discuss with the team; do not automatically give an anticholinergic.
2. **Lithium plus vomiting and new unsteadiness.** Check hydration, renal/interacting-drug changes and last-dose timing; escalate possible toxicity promptly. A previously reassuring level does not settle today's presentation.
3. **Clozapine and “just constipation.”** Abdominal pain, distension, vomiting or reduced bowel movements may signal dangerous hypomotility. Obtain urgent assessment rather than treating it as a minor comfort issue.
4. **Lamotrigine after an interruption.** The home list shows the old regimen, but treatment has been missed. Verify the interruption and product-specific restart plan; do not assume resuming the old regimen is safe.
5. **Scheduled lorazepam at discharge.** Is the indication catatonia, withdrawal, another condition, or an unreconciled PRN? Clarify benefit, dependence risk, follow-up and supervised continuation/taper. Neither automatic renewal nor abrupt cessation is a safe default.

**Practice the conversation:** “Which matters most right now: symptom relief, staying alert, sleep, weight, sexual effects, or how easy the plan is to follow?” Rehearse [medication ambivalence about weight gain](?tool=communication-practice.html&case=medication_ambivalence_001).

## High-yield pearls

- Match bipolar treatment to the illness phase; a maintenance role does not establish acute antimanic efficacy.
- Compare prior response, adverse effects, patient priorities and formulation when choosing an antipsychotic.
- Lithium needs renal/thyroid monitoring and timed levels; do not promise suicide prevention.
- Benzodiazepines require a specific indication and reassessment; avoid abrupt cessation when physical dependence is likely.
- Clozapine requires ongoing ANC monitoring and attention to bowel function, myocarditis, smoking changes and interrupted treatment.
